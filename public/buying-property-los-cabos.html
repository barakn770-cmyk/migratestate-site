Buying Property in Los Cabos as an American (2026): Fideicomiso, Resort Communities, Costs and Tax

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated October 2026  ·  14 min read
Cabo San Lucas, the Corridor, and San José del Cabo are inside Mexico's restricted zone, so an American holds a coastal home through a fideicomiso. The Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores lists MXN 21,650 as the 2026 fee to issue that permit. The Municipality of Los Cabos charges its own acquisition tax at 3% of value after a UMA reduction, on the highest of price, cadastral value, or appraisal. The appraisal is good for 90 days. Short-let charges are statutory and separate from that 3%. Resort rules, hurricane cover, and any dollar payment plan from a developer are contracts, not rates in the tax law.

Los Cabos is sold as one market: Cabo San Lucas, the tourist corridor, and San José del Cabo, priced in dollars and managed like a resort. The tax that actually hits the deed is narrower than that marketing. It is a municipal acquisition tax, written for the Municipality of Los Cabos, not a single Baja California Sur rate you can copy from a state map. The ownership wrapper on the coast is the same one that applies in Puerto Vallarta and Tulum: a bank trust, because the beach is inside the restricted zone. The national mechanics are in the Mexico buying guide and the fideicomiso guide. This page is the Los Cabos statute, the resort paperwork the statute does not set, and the US return that still applies after you close.

The coast is in the restricted zone

Article 27 of the Constitution bars a foreigner from holding direct title to residential land within 50 kilometers of the coast or 100 kilometers of a border. Cabo San Lucas, the Corridor, and San José del Cabo are on the water. An American buyer of a house or condo there holds it through a fideicomiso, a bank trust, after a permit from the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores. The SRE cost page, marked as information in force from 1 January 2026 and sourced by the SRE to Anexo 19 of the 2026 miscellaneous tax resolution, lists MXN 21,650 to issue the permit to constitute a fideicomiso in the restricted zone. The same page lists a separate fee to modify a permit and another to extend the term. The permit page itself describes a maximum term of 50 years, residential use, and a contract that must be a public deed.

A lot farther inland in the same municipality can sit outside the 50-kilometer strip. That is a measurement, not a slogan. If the land is outside the strip, a foreigner can take direct title after the SRE's waiver of the constitutional restriction, which is a different filing and a different fee on that same cost page. If the land is inside the strip, the fideicomiso is the path. Ask the notario to state the distance in the file. Do not let a listing decide it.

The municipal 3% acquisition tax

Los Cabos does not use a state-wide acquisition-tax schedule. Article 35 of the Ley de Hacienda para el Municipio de Los Cabos (the municipal treasury's text, last reform noted in that file as 27 December 2024) says the tax is calculated by applying 3% to the value of the property after reducing that value by five times the daily UMA, raised to a year. The statute's phrase is "cinco veces al valor diario de la Unidad de Medida y Actualización, elevado al año." Article 41 says the daily UMA used for that reduction is the one for the calendar year of the payment event in article 38. Article 42 limits how many times you can take the reduction when you buy adjoining parcels within 24 months, and it cuts the reduction in half for a bare usufruct or bare title.

INEGI's 8 January 2026 bulletin sets the UMA in force from 1 February 2026 at MXN 117.31 a day, MXN 3,566.22 a month, and MXN 42,794.64 a year. This page does not turn "elevado al año" into a single peso deduction. The annual UMA INEGI publishes is the daily value times 30.4, times 12. A reading that multiplies the daily value by 365 is a few pesos different. The notario computes articles 41 and 42. Five times the published annual UMA is on the order of MXN 214,000 of value, which is small next to a Corridor purchase and is not a foreigner discount. Everyone who owes the ordinary rate gets the same reduction.

Article 37 defines the value the 3% hits. It is the highest of the price in the contract, the cadastral or fiscal value, and a commercial appraisal by a perito authorized by the municipality. That appraisal is valid for no more than 90 calendar days from the day it was issued. A Quintana Roo buyer who just read the Tulum guide should not reuse that state's 180-day appraisal window. Los Cabos wrote 90.

When the tax is due, and what the registry will not do

Article 38 requires payment within 30 days of the transaction, at the recaudación for the place where the property sits. For a fideicomiso the article is specific. The tax can be triggered when a preparatory private contract transmits the property to the trustee, or to a beneficiary who is not the settlor, or when the public deed transmits the property to the trustee. The notario calculates the tax, writes it into the deed, and remits it. Article 40 says the Registro Público de la Propiedad will not inscribe a transfer without proof the tax was paid, or proof it was not owed.

The 2026 municipal income law, published in the state official gazette on 24 December 2025, still books "impuesto sobre adquisición de bienes inmuebles" as a revenue line for this municipality for the whole of 2026. It does not replace article 35. The rate you budget is the hacienda-law rate, not a number inferred from that revenue estimate.

On that MXN 10,000,000 example, 3% before the reduction is MXN 300,000. The reduction comes off the value, so it lowers the tax by 3% of whatever peso amount articles 41 and 42 produce. It does not wipe out a resort purchase. Do not convert the example into dollars on this page. Use the day's rate, and use the appraisal if it is higher than the contract.

The 3% is the easy line. The zone and the reglamento are the file.

A notario who is not the seller's notario should confirm the 50-kilometer measurement, the 90-day appraisal, and what the community's own rules do to a rental you are counting on.

Resort communities, HOAs, and rental pools

Most of what buyers mean by "Los Cabos rules" is not in the hacienda law. Master-planned communities and condo towers bind owners through a recorded condominium reglamento and a set of covenants: assessments, architectural control, a rental pool, a minimum stay, a ban on platforms, a requirement that rentals run through the hotel operator. Those documents can prohibit the short-let income a listing described. They can also require you to carry the building's insurance deductible. None of that is a tax rate, and this page will not invent an HOA percentage. Read the reglamento and the last assembly minutes before you treat nightly income as a feature of the unit.

A rental-pool contract is a second document. It can assign your weeks, set a fee, and limit personal use. It is not the deed and it is not the fideicomiso. If the pool pays you in dollars into a US account, that is still rental income for the US return described below. If it pays you into a Mexican account, that account can be an FBAR account. The pool agreement does not turn the house into something the FBAR ignores, and it does not turn the house into an FBAR asset by itself. The account does.

Short-let charges: municipal fee and state tax

If you do rent, budget two different government charges, and do not add them into the 3%.

The municipal environmental sanitation fee is in the same Los Cabos hacienda law, articles 137 Bis through 137 Quater. It is charged because a room is occupied. Hotels, motels, guesthouses, and hostels owe 70% of the daily UMA per occupied room, withheld by the operator when the guest pays. Stays sold through digital platforms and similar channels owe twice the daily UMA per occupied room, house, or apartment. With INEGI's daily UMA of MXN 117.31 from 1 February 2026, twice the daily UMA is MXN 234.62. Seventy percent of 117.31 pesos is the hotel amount. The statute does not reprint it as a rounded peso figure, so this page does not round it either. Article 137 Bis 1 says the money is for beaches, wastewater, waste, and related municipal work. It is not the acquisition tax, and it is not optional fine print on a platform listing.

The state lodging tax is a different statute. Articles 13 to 16 of the Ley de Hacienda del Estado de Baja California Sur, in the finance secretariat's compiled PDF (that file notes a last reform of 31 December 2017), cover hotels, motels, guesthouses, camps, RV parks, tourist marinas, timeshares, and other lodging. Article 16 of that compilation says the tax is 3% of the base in article 15. The state's own trámites page for pago del impuesto sobre la prestación de servicios de hospedaje says the tax is calculated at 4%. Both pages are official. They do not match. This guide does not pick one. Confirm the rate in force with the Secretaría de Finanzas de Baja California Sur before you model short-let income. The 2026 state income law still lists the lodging tax as a revenue line. It does not, in the text reviewed here, resolve the 3% versus 4% conflict.

Water, permits, and hurricane cover

Los Cabos is a desert coast. Article 64 Bis of the municipal hacienda law requires, before the municipality will take a filing for a building permit, an enlargement, a demolition, a subdivision, a land-use opinion, or a commercial license, proof that predial is current and that the property is on the cadastre, plus a certificate that water, sewer, and wastewater discharge are in order. A new water or sewer connection has the same predial prerequisite. That is the diligence item. This page does not quote a desalination tariff or a connection fee, because the acquisition-tax statute does not set them. Ask the municipal water utility for the account and the certificate. A brochure sentence about "the desal plant" is not that certificate.

Cabo San Lucas and San José del Cabo sit on the Pacific hurricane coast. No article in the hacienda law sets an insurance premium, a deductible, or a requirement that a foreign buyer buy a named policy. Insurance is a contract. Read the community's master policy, see what it does not cover, and price a separate policy if the master policy leaves the interior or the loss of rent to you. Do not budget a percentage you found in a listing.

Developer dollar plans are contracts

Some Los Cabos developers offer a payment schedule denominated in dollars. That is a contract with the developer. It is not a mortgage rate published by Banco de México, and it is not the SRE fee. This page does not quote an interest rate, a down payment, or a term for those plans, because no official schedule reviewed for this article sets one. Who lends to foreigners, and how a US return treats mortgage interest, is in the Mexico mortgage guide. If you later repay a peso or dollar loan with a different number of dollars than you borrowed, IRC Section 988 can tax the currency result. That is a US rule, and it is not cancelled by the fact the developer called the plan "financing."

Pre-construction deposits are in the same category. Mexican law does not create a statutory construction escrow for every beach project. If the contract does not put the deposit in an account your own notario can see, the deposit is the developer's money. Confirm title, the building permit, and the water certificate in the previous section before the first wire.

US tax: Revenue Ruling 2013-14

IRS Revenue Ruling 2013-14 holds that a standard Mexican fideicomiso used to hold a personal residence is not a trust for United States tax purposes. Forms 3520 and 3520-A are not required for that arrangement. It applies in Los Cabos the same way it applies anywhere else in the restricted zone. Rent is still US income. How a foreign residential rental is depreciated is in the 30-year ADS guide. Mexico-specific reporting is in US taxes on Mexican property. A sale has its own Mexican withholding, covered in selling property in Mexico as a US citizen.

The house is not an FBAR asset. A Mexican bank account used to buy it, collect rent, or pay the HOA can be. The FBAR line is $10,000 aggregate, at any point in the year. Form 8938 starts higher and depends on where you live and how you file. Both are explained in the FBAR guide and the Form 8938 guide. Buying the condo does not make you a Mexican resident. Residency is a separate filing. The income and savings tests are in the temporary resident visa guide.

Step-by-step

  1. Hire your own notario, not the seller's and not the developer's, before you sign.
  2. Measure the restricted zone. Coast inside 50 kilometers means a fideicomiso. Outside it, the path is direct title plus the SRE waiver, which is a different fee.
  3. Read the reglamento and any rental-pool contract before you count on short-let income.
  4. Order a municipal-authorized appraisal and check the date. Article 37 gives it 90 days.
  5. Open the fideicomiso and pay the SRE's 2026 issuance fee of MXN 21,650.
  6. Budget the 3% on the highest of price, cadastral value, or appraisal, after the article 35 reduction the notario computes, and pay within 30 days.
  7. For a build or a renovation, collect the predial receipt and the water and discharge certificates article 64 Bis asks for before the permit filing.
  8. If you will live there, start residency separately. The deed is not a visa.

If you're not American

Article 27, the SRE permit, the Los Cabos 3% acquisition tax, the 90-day appraisal, the municipal sanitation fee, and the state lodging tax apply to a foreign buyer regardless of passport. Revenue Ruling 2013-14, the FBAR, and Form 8938 are United States rules. If you are not a US person, ask an adviser in your own country whether a Mexican fideicomiso is a trust there, and whether a Mexican bank account is reportable at home.

Frequently asked questions

Can an American own property in Los Cabos?

On the coast, yes, through a fideicomiso. Article 27 of the Constitution bars a foreigner from holding direct title to residential land within 50 kilometers of the sea. Cabo San Lucas, the Corridor, and San José del Cabo sit inside that strip. The buyer applies through a notario and a Mexican bank for a permit from the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores. The SRE's own 2026 fee schedule, in force from 1 January 2026, lists MXN 21,650 to issue that permit. A parcel farther inland in the municipality can fall outside the strip. Measure the distance. Do not take a sales office's word for it.

What transfer tax do I pay in Los Cabos?

The Municipality of Los Cabos charges its own impuesto sobre adquisición de inmuebles under article 35 of the Ley de Hacienda para el Municipio de Los Cabos. The ordinary rate is 3% of the property's value after that value is reduced by five times the daily UMA, raised to a year. The value is the highest of the contract price, the cadastral or fiscal value, and a commercial appraisal by a municipally authorized appraiser. That appraisal is valid for 90 days, not 180. On an illustrative value of MXN 10,000,000, 3% before the reduction is MXN 300,000. Family gifts and inheritances in the direct line use 1.5% after a larger reduction. There is no foreigner surcharge in the statute.

When is the Los Cabos acquisition tax due on a fideicomiso?

Article 38 says the tax is paid within 30 days of the transaction, at the local tax office. For a fideicomiso, the trigger can be the preparatory private contract that transmits the property to the trustee bank or to a beneficiary other than the settlor, or the public deed that transmits the property to the trustee, whichever comes first among the cases the article lists. The public registry will not record a transfer without proof the tax was paid or that it was not owed.

Do resort HOA rules in Los Cabos come from a state statute?

The acquisition tax does. The house rules of a master-planned community do not. Short-let bans, rental pools, and assessment formulas live in the recorded condominium reglamento and the community covenants. Read those documents. A rental-pool contract is a separate agreement from the deed.

What short-let taxes apply in Los Cabos?

Two different charges exist, and they are easy to mix up. The municipality's environmental sanitation fee, in the same hacienda law, is 70% of the daily UMA per occupied hotel room, and twice the daily UMA per occupied room, house, or apartment when the stay is sold through a digital platform. INEGI's daily UMA from 1 February 2026 is MXN 117.31, so the platform amount is MXN 234.62. Separately, the state lodging tax covers hotels, timeshares, and other lodging. The finance secretariat's compiled state hacienda law sets that tax at 3% in article 16. The state's own trámites page for the same tax states 4%. Those two official pages disagree, so this guide does not quote a single lodging-tax rate.

Does Revenue Ruling 2013-14 cover a Los Cabos fideicomiso?

Yes. IRS Revenue Ruling 2013-14 treats a standard Mexican fideicomiso that holds a personal residence as not a trust for US tax purposes, so Forms 3520 and 3520-A are not required for that arrangement. Rent and a later sale are still reported on the US return. A Mexican bank account can trigger the FBAR once foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any point in the year.

Does buying in Los Cabos give me Mexican residency?

No. The deed and the SRE permit are not a visa. Residency is a separate application, most often a temporary resident visa, and it does not change how the property is titled.

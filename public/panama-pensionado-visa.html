Panama Pensionado Visa for Americans (2026): Requirements, Discounts and the Property Alternative

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated September 2026  ·  11 min read
Panama's Pensionado visa is permanent residency from the day it's approved — no two-year provisional stage. The standard threshold is a guaranteed lifetime pension of US$1,000/month (US Social Security qualifies), reduced to US$750/month if you also own titled Panamanian real estate worth more than US$100,000. It comes with a statutory discount package — 50% off entertainment, 25% off airfare, 15–25% off dining, medical, transport and utilities — under Law 6 of 1987. Not retired? Panama's Friendly Nations Visa is the alternative, covered below.

Panama has run some version of a retiree residency program since the 1980s, and it remains, by a wide margin, the easiest route into permanent residency the country offers — easier than the property-and-deposit-based Friendly Nations Visa, easier than employment-based permits, and unlike almost every other "retire here" program in Latin America, it grants permanent status the moment it is approved, not a provisional card that converts later. This guide covers exactly what qualifies, what it costs, the statutory discount list most guides only summarize, and the one thing worth deciding before you file: whether Pensionado or the Friendly Nations Visa fits your actual situation.

What the Pensionado visa is

The Permiso de Residente Permanente en Calidad de Jubilado o Pensionado — the Pensionado visa — is a special-policy permanent residence category for foreigners who receive a guaranteed lifetime pension. It sits alongside the Friendly Nations Visa and several other categories under Panama's immigration framework (Decree-Law 3 of 2008 and its regulations), administered by the Servicio Nacional de Migración (SNM). Unlike almost every investment-based residency program, Pensionado does not require you to buy anything, deposit anything, or create a company — it requires only proof that a qualifying pension will keep paying you for life.

Do Americans qualify?

Yes, and there is no nationality restriction on the Pensionado category the way there is on the Friendly Nations Visa's country list — any foreigner from any country can apply, provided they meet the pension requirement. There is also no minimum age: the requirement is about the nature of the pension (guaranteed, lifetime), not the applicant's birthdate, so a younger applicant on a qualifying disability or early-retirement pension can apply as readily as someone collecting Social Security at 65. US Social Security retirement benefits are the single most common qualifying source for American applicants precisely because they are lifetime and guaranteed by design — a private pension, a government pension (including military and federal-employee pensions), or a qualifying annuity can also work if the paying institution will certify that it is guaranteed for life, not a fixed term or a balance that can be exhausted.

The $1,000 rule — and the $750 property alternative

Panama's National Immigration Service publishes the pension thresholds directly on its official Jubilado/Pensionado requirements sheet:

RouteMonthly pension requiredExtra condition
StandardB/.1,000 (US$1,000)None — pension alone qualifies
Property-reducedB/.750 (US$750)Must also hold titled Panamanian real estate, in your personal name, worth more than B/.100,000
Dependents+B/.250/month eachAdditional proven pension or income per dependent added to the application

The Panamanian balboa is pegged 1:1 to the US dollar and the two circulate interchangeably, so "B/." and "US$" figures here are the same number. Note the property-reduced route's fine print: the real estate must be titled and held in your own name — the same "Rights of Possession" land that does not qualify for Panama's Friendly Nations property route also will not satisfy this rule, and the property must exceed B/.100,000 in registered value, not merely be listed at that price. Combining a qualifying pension with a Panamanian property purchase this way can meaningfully lower the ongoing pension requirement, which matters for applicants whose Social Security or private pension sits in the $750–$999 range.

The statutory discount list

The Pensionado visa's other headline feature is a legally mandated discount package under Law 6 of 16 June 1987 (as amended by Laws 18/1989, 15/1992, 37/2001 and 14/2003) — not marketing perks a business can withdraw, but statutory rights that carry fines of B/.50–B/.5,000 for a business that refuses to honor them. The law requires "special windows" and priority service at public and private service points. The core list:

A few practical notes the marketing pages skip: the restaurant discount excludes small unlicensed "fondas," the entertainment discount excludes charitable events for children or disaster victims, and every discount applies to your personal consumption — it is not transferable to a group bill or a non-Pensionado spouse's separate purchase unless they hold their own qualifying dependent status. Carry your Pensionado ID (cédula) at all times; the discount is claimed at the point of sale, not refunded after the fact.

Permanent from day one — the real structural advantage

This is the detail that separates Pensionado from every other Panama residency route: it was never touched by the 2021 reform (Executive Decree 197) that added a two-year provisional stage to the Friendly Nations Visa. Pensionado applicants who are approved receive a permanent residency card and Panamanian ID (cédula E) directly — there is no renewal requirement and no second filing years later. The file itself is typically resolved within a few months, dependent on SNM's current caseload and the completeness of your pension documentation.

The US tax angle

Panama runs a territorial tax system: it taxes only Panama-source income and does not tax foreign-source pension income at all, which is a large part of Pensionado's appeal. For a US citizen, that exemption is close to irrelevant to your actual tax bill. The United States taxes citizens on worldwide income regardless of where they live, so your Social Security or pension income stays fully US-taxable on exactly the same terms as if you had stayed home — becoming a Panamanian resident, or even a genuine Panamanian tax resident, changes nothing about the IRS side of that income. If Panama does not tax it, there is also no foreign tax paid to claim as a credit, so residency in a zero-tax country can, in a narrow sense, leave you with no offset at all — though since Panama was never going to tax the pension either way, there is no double taxation to relieve in the first place.

Two reporting points matter more than the headline "no Panama tax" claim: if you open a Panamanian bank account (common for receiving pension deposits locally or paying utilities at their discounted rate), it is a foreign financial account, and once your aggregate foreign accounts exceed US$10,000 at any point in the year you must file an FBAR (FinCEN Form 114), with Form 8938 (FATCA) potentially applying above higher thresholds. And if you buy the B/.100,000+ property to unlock the reduced $750 threshold, that purchase follows the same US rules as any foreign real estate purchase — see our guides to US taxes on foreign property and FBAR for foreign property owners for the reporting mechanics, which apply the same way to a Panamanian purchase.

Not retired? The Friendly Nations Visa alternative

Pensionado only works if you already have a qualifying guaranteed lifetime pension. If you are not yet retired, are self-employed without a pension, or simply do not want to document a lifetime pension stream, Panama's Friendly Nations Visa is the standard alternative for Americans: a US$200,000 titled property or bank-deposit threshold (or a genuine Panamanian job), open to citizens of roughly 50 listed countries including the United States. The trade-off runs the other way from Pensionado's: a materially higher capital requirement, but no pension to prove — and, since the 2021 reform, a two-year provisional stage before a separate permanent-residency filing, the opposite of Pensionado's immediate permanent status. Many MigrateState readers who are still working compare the two routes side by side with an immigration attorney before deciding, since the "right" one depends entirely on whether a qualifying pension already exists.

Step-by-step: applying as an American

  1. Confirm your pension qualifies. Get a certification from the paying institution (Social Security Administration, a private pension administrator, or a government pension office) stating the benefit is guaranteed and lifetime, at or above the B/.1,000 (or B/.750-with-property) threshold.
  2. Decide whether the property route helps. If your pension sits below B/.1,000/month but at or above B/.750, a titled Panamanian property purchase over B/.100,000 can unlock the reduced threshold — weigh that against simply waiting or supplementing the pension amount.
  3. Assemble and apostille your documents. Passport, police-clearance certificate, health certificate, pension certification, and (for dependents) proof of the additional B/.250/month each — every US-issued document needs the full apostille chain and, typically, certified Spanish translation.
  4. Engage a Panamanian immigration attorney to prepare and file the application with SNM under power of attorney.
  5. Appear in Panama for biometrics and any required interview.
  6. Receive your permanent residency card and cédula E — no provisional stage, no later renewal filing.
  7. Register your Pensionado status with participating merchants, airlines and utilities to start claiming the Law 6 discounts.

If you're not American: what changes

The Panama side of Pensionado — the B/.1,000/B/.750 thresholds, the property alternative, the Law 6 discounts, and permanent status from day one — is identical for any nationality, unlike the Friendly Nations Visa's country list. Only your home-country tax treatment differs.

If you are not a US citizen, the entire Panama-side process above stays as written — nothing about qualifying, filing, or the discount list changes based on your passport. Swap the US-specific tax section for your own country's rules and check three things with a tax adviser where you're resident: (1) whether your home country taxes foreign-source pension income while you hold Panamanian residency, and on what basis (many tax on residence rather than citizenship, so genuinely relocating can reduce your home-country exposure in a way it never can for a US citizen); (2) whether you must report foreign accounts or assets at home if you open a Panamanian bank account; and (3) whether a double-taxation treaty exists between Panama and your country — Panama has relatively few. Confirm your specific position before you rely on Panama's territorial exemption meaning anything back home.

Is the Pensionado visa worth it in 2026?

For anyone who already has a qualifying guaranteed pension — and for the large number of Americans on Social Security, that is a low bar — Pensionado is arguably the single best-value residency-by-retirement program available anywhere: no investment required, a genuinely useful statutory discount package with real enforcement teeth, and permanent status from the moment your file is approved rather than a multi-year provisional wait. It is a weaker fit if your pension income sits below B/.750/month and you are not prepared to also buy Panamanian property, or if you are not yet drawing a lifetime pension at all — in which case the Friendly Nations Visa, not Pensionado, is the route to compare against Panama's property market. Either way, Panama's territorial tax system is a genuine local benefit that does nothing to your IRS return, so build the US tax side into the decision from the start rather than discovering it after you've relocated.

Frequently asked questions

How much pension do I need for the Panama Pensionado visa?

A guaranteed lifetime pension of at least B/.1,000 (US$1,000) per month, confirmed on Panama's National Immigration Service official requirements sheet. That drops to B/.750 per month if you also hold titled Panamanian real estate worth more than B/.100,000 in your personal name. Dependents need an additional B/.250 per month of proven income or pension each.

Is the Panama Pensionado visa permanent residency right away?

Yes. Unlike Panama's Friendly Nations Visa, which now starts with a two-year provisional stage, the Pensionado permit is issued as permanent residency from approval, with no renewal requirement and no waiting period before it converts to permanent status.

What discounts does the Panama Pensionado visa give you?

Law 6 of 16 June 1987 grants a statutory discount list: 50% off entertainment (cinemas, theaters, sporting events), 25% off airline tickets, 30% off buses, trains and ferries, 25% off restaurant meals (15% at fast-food chains), 15–20% off medical, dental and optical services, 50%/30% off hotel stays (weekday/weekend), 25% off electricity and water up to a monthly cap, and a frozen property tax rate on your single residence, among others.

Can Social Security qualify me for the Pensionado visa?

Yes. US Social Security retirement benefits are the single most common qualifying pension source for American Pensionado applicants, since they are guaranteed and lifetime by design. A qualifying private or government pension can also work if the paying institution will certify it is lifetime.

Does Pensionado lead to Panamanian citizenship?

General Panamanian naturalization requires five years of permanent residency plus a Spanish-language and civics assessment, and Pensionado permit holders are generally understood to be treated the same as other permanent residents for that purpose. That said, this guide could not confirm on an official source whether SNM applies the Pensionado category identically to every other permanent-residency route in practice — confirm your specific eligibility with a Panamanian immigration attorney once you approach the five-year mark.

What if I'm not retired — is there a Panama residency option?

Yes. Panama's Friendly Nations Visa is the standard route for Americans who are not living on a qualifying pension — it uses a US$200,000 property or bank-deposit threshold (or a Panamanian job), but runs on a two-year provisional-to-permanent timeline rather than the Pensionado's immediate permanent status.

Do Americans pay US tax on a Panama pension?

Yes, in full. The US taxes citizens on worldwide income regardless of residency, so Social Security or pension income stays fully US-taxable whether you live in Panama or not. Panama's territorial tax system means it does not tax your foreign-source pension — but that exemption is Panama's, not the IRS's, and does nothing to reduce your US return.

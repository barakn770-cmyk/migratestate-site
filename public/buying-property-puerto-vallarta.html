Buying Property in Puerto Vallarta as an American (2026): Fideicomiso, Zones, Condo Rules and Tax

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated October 2026  ·  12 min read
Puerto Vallarta and Nuevo Vallarta are both inside Mexico’s restricted zone, so an American holds the condo in a fideicomiso. The SRE’s 2026 fee to issue that permit is $21,650. Puerto Vallarta’s municipal transfer tax on a MXN 5,000,000 base is MXN 147,729.54. Across the state line, Bahía de Banderas charges 2%, which is MXN 100,000 on the same base. The lodging-tax rate in both state income laws for 2026 is 5%, not 3%.

Puerto Vallarta is a Jalisco city. Nuevo Vallarta and Bucerías are a few minutes north, in Bahía de Banderas, Nayarit. Listings treat them as one beach market. The statutes do not. The national rules — who can hold title, and how a foreigner buys inside the coastal strip — are in our guide to buying property in Mexico. This page is the local layer: the 2026 municipal transfer taxes, the state lodging taxes, and the permit fee a buyer actually pays to set up the trust.

One coast, two states, both restricted

Article 27 of the Constitution bars foreigners from holding direct title to land and waters within 50 kilometers of the coast. Puerto Vallarta sits on that coast. So do Nuevo Vallarta and Bucerías. A residential purchase in either place is held through a fideicomiso, a bank trust, not in the buyer’s name. The national mechanics, including the renewable term, are in the fideicomiso guide.

The fideicomiso and the $21,650 permit

The buyer applies, through the notario and the bank, for a permit from the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores to constitute the trust in the restricted zone. The SRE’s own cost and timing page lists $21,650 for issuing that permit. The 2026 Ley Federal de Derechos annex shows why the two figures that circulate are a peso apart: Article 25, fraction V, paragraph a) is $21,648.83 before the annual adjustment and $21,650 after it. Pay the adjusted figure the SRE publishes. The same annex lists an adjusted $9,740 to modify a permit (fraction V, paragraph b; unadjusted $9,740.31) and an adjusted $10,615 for a late renewal (paragraph c; unadjusted $10,613.78).

That fee is the government charge for the permit. It is not the bank’s setup fee and it is not the annual trustee fee. Those are in the trust contract. The fideicomiso guide walks through what to ask the bank. Financing, if a lender will do it at all, is a separate question covered in getting a mortgage in Mexico.

Transfer tax: Vallarta’s tariff vs 2% in Bahía de Banderas

Puerto Vallarta does not charge a flat state percentage. The 2026 municipal income law — decreto of the state congress número 30058/LXIV/25, Artículo 41 — taxes transmissions of real property. The base is the higher of (a) an appraisal by an authorized expert approved by the Dirección de Catastro or (b) the price in the contract or the aviso. The tax is ((BI − LI) × T) + CF, using this tariff:

A base of MXN 5,000,000 falls in the 4,067,064.01–6,559,136.00 bracket. The tax is 118,342.06 + (5,000,000 − 4,067,064.01) × 0.0315 = MXN 147,729.54, about 2.95% of that base. Listings are often written in dollars. The statute is in pesos, and the notario puts the contract price into this peso base. This page does not convert the example at a guessed exchange rate.

Bahía de Banderas uses a different statute: the Ley de Ingresos del Municipio de Bahía de Banderas for 2026, published in the state official paper on 29 December 2025. Artículo 21 sets ISAI at 2% of the highest of the contract price, the commercial appraisal, and the cadastral appraisal. Social or popular housing is 0.50%, and the tax cannot be less than 7.8674 UMA. On the same MXN 5,000,000 base the 2% tax is MXN 100,000. Puerto Vallarta’s bill on that base is MXN 47,729.54 higher. Do not use a Tepic municipal income law for this number. Tepic is a different municipality, and Nayarit does not set one statewide ISAI rate in the Bahía de Banderas law.

Jalisco’s state income law also has a chapter on transmissions of movable property that mentions constitutive acts of a fideicomiso. This page does not treat that chapter as a second tax on the Vallarta condo. The transfer tax a buyer should budget is the municipal one in Article 41, calculated by the notario on the peso base above.

Lodging tax is 5% in both states

A yield spreadsheet that still uses 3% for Jalisco is using a retired rate. Jalisco’s 2026 income law, Article 12, sets the impuesto sobre hospedaje at 5.0%. The base is the one in the state hacienda law. Article 47 of that law taxes lodging income in timeshares, hotels, motels, camps and RV parks, and Article 51 says the rate is whatever the income law sets. An ordinary residential condo offered by the night is not automatically inside that building list. Read the chapter against the actual use before you put 5% into the model.

Nayarit’s 2026 income law, Article 4, sets the lodging tax at 5% of the base in the state hacienda law. The income law points at that hacienda law for what is included. This page does not claim that every Nayarit condo short-let pays it.

The rate depends on which side of the bridge the building sits on.

A notario in Puerto Vallarta and a notario in Bahía de Banderas will not hand you the same transfer-tax bill. Have one of them run Article 41 or Article 21 on your actual peso base before you wire a deposit.

Condo rules, deposits, and the Pacific season

The registered condominium reglamento can prohibit short lets even when a platform will take the listing. Read the reglamento, and the last assembly minutes, before you treat nightly income as a feature of the unit. The restriction, when it exists, is in that building’s own rules.

On a pre-construction contract, a deposit paid to the developer is the developer’s money unless the contract puts it somewhere the buyer and the notario can see. The SRE permit does not hold the deposit. The notario should also confirm, on the public registry, that the land under the project is titled to the party the contract names.

Puerto Vallarta and Bahía de Banderas face the Pacific hurricane season. No statute on this page sets an insurance premium. Ask for the building’s elevation, the last assessment, and a quote before you treat the HOA fee as the whole carrying cost.

Predial and what not to invent

Predial, the annual property tax, is municipal. Puerto Vallarta and Bahía de Banderas each bill it on their own cadastral value. Ask for the current bill rather than a percentage copied from a national overview. Notario fees are a professional tariff, not a line in Article 41 or Article 21, so this page does not quote one.

US tax: Revenue Ruling 2013-14

IRS Revenue Ruling 2013-14 holds that a standard Mexican fideicomiso used to hold a personal residence is not a trust for United States tax purposes. Forms 3520 and 3520-A are not required for that arrangement. If you rent the condo, the income is still reportable in the United States. How foreign residential property is depreciated is covered in the depreciation guide, and the Mexico-specific reporting is in US taxes on Mexican property. Selling has its own withholding rules, in selling property in Mexico as a US citizen.

A Mexican bank account is not the house. If your foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any point in the year, the FBAR is in play. The purchase itself does not make you a Mexican resident. Residency is a separate application, described in the temporary resident visa guide.

Step-by-step

  1. Confirm the municipality. Puerto Vallarta and Bahía de Banderas do not share a transfer-tax statute. The listing name “Vallarta” is not the law.
  2. Hire your own notario and have the peso base appraised under the rule that municipality actually uses.
  3. Read the reglamento and, on a new building, where the deposit sits. The SRE permit is not an escrow.
  4. Open the fideicomiso with a Mexican bank and pay the SRE issuance fee of $21,650.
  5. Close and register. Keep the closing statement for your US cost basis.
  6. If you will live there, start the residency file separately. The deed is not a visa.

If you're not American

Article 27, the SRE permit, Puerto Vallarta’s Article 41 tariff, and Bahía de Banderas’ 2% ISAI apply to a foreign buyer regardless of passport. A Canadian or a British buyer uses the same trust and the same municipal math. What changes is the home-country tax. Revenue Ruling 2013-14 and the FBAR are United States rules. If you are not a US person, ask an adviser in your own country whether the fideicomiso is a trust there, and whether a Mexican bank account is reportable at home.

Frequently asked questions

Can an American buy property in Puerto Vallarta?

Yes, but not by taking the deed in your own name. Puerto Vallarta is on the Pacific coast, inside the restricted zone in Article 27 of the Mexican Constitution: within 50 kilometers of the coast a foreigner cannot hold direct title to residential land. The purchase is held in a fideicomiso, a bank trust, after a permit from the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores. Nuevo Vallarta and Bucerías, across the state line in Bahía de Banderas, Nayarit, are on the same coast and use the same trust. Buying does not by itself give you the right to live in Mexico.

What does the SRE charge for a fideicomiso permit in 2026?

The SRE fee schedule lists $21,650 for issuing the permit to constitute a fideicomiso in the restricted zone. The 2026 Ley Federal de Derechos annex lists the unadjusted amount for Article 25, fraction V, paragraph a) as $21,648.83 and the adjusted amount as $21,650. The same annex lists an adjusted $9,740 to modify a permit and $10,615 for a late renewal. Bank trustee fees are not on that schedule.

How is the transfer tax different in Puerto Vallarta and Bahía de Banderas?

Puerto Vallarta’s 2026 income law, Article 41, taxes the higher of an authorized catastro appraisal and the contract price on a progressive tariff. On a base of MXN 5,000,000 the tax is MXN 147,729.54. Bahía de Banderas’ 2026 income law, Article 21, charges 2% on the highest of the contract price, the commercial appraisal, and the cadastral appraisal, which is MXN 100,000 on the same base. Social or popular housing there is 0.50%, with a minimum of 7.8674 UMA. A foreign condo is not that housing.

Is the lodging tax on a Vallarta rental 3%?

No. Jalisco’s 2026 income law, Article 12, sets the lodging tax at 5.0% of the base in the state hacienda law. Nayarit’s 2026 income law, Article 4, sets the same tax at 5% of the base in Nayarit’s hacienda law. Jalisco’s hacienda law, Article 47, defines the object as lodging income in timeshares, hotels, motels, camps and RV parks. An ordinary residential condo let by the night is not automatically on that list. Confirm the chapter applies before you put 5% into a yield model.

Is a Mexican fideicomiso a foreign trust on my US return?

IRS Revenue Ruling 2013-14 holds that a standard Mexican fideicomiso used to hold a personal residence is not a trust for US tax purposes, so Forms 3520 and 3520-A are not required for that arrangement. Rental income and a later sale are still US-taxable. A Mexican bank account can be an FBAR account if your foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any point in the year.

Does buying a condo give me Mexican residency?

No. The deed and the SRE permit are not a visa. Residency is a separate application, usually a temporary resident visa.

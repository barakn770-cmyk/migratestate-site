Greece Digital Nomad Visa for Americans (2026)

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Updated November 2026 · 12 min read
Greece's digital nomad route is a national entry visa for remote work, not a permit to take a Greek job. Article 68 of Law 5038/2023 lets a third-country national who works, by information and communication technology, for an employer or clients outside Greece apply for residence title Z.1. The consulate can issue the national visa for up to 12 months. Sufficient resources are €3,500 a month, and for dependent work that figure is net of tax in the country where the work is done. A spouse or cohabiting partner adds 20%, and each child adds 15%. The national-visa fee in the article is €75.

Americans who want to live in Greece while keeping a job, a freelance practice, or a company that is not Greek often get pointed at the wrong permit. The financially independent person permit is a non-work category. The Golden Visa is a property investment. Neither is written for a person whose income is a salary or invoices from outside Greece. Article 68 of the Immigration Code is the provision that is. This guide stays inside the sentences of that article, the joint ministerial decision that later fixed how "sufficient resources" are read, the finance ministry's description of who is taxed in Greece, and the IRS pages on citizens abroad and on the FBAR. Where a later paragraph of the code was not in the text read for this page, the page says so instead of filling the gap.

What the visa actually is

Law 5038/2023, the Code of Immigration and Social Integration, published in the Government Gazette on 1 April 2023, creates a residence title called Z.1 for digital nomads. Article 68 addresses third-country nationals who are self-employed, freelancers, or employees, and who can do their work remotely, using information and communication technologies, for employers or clients outside Greece. The competent consular authority grants those people a national entry visa for a period of up to twelve months.

The same paragraph tells the consulate how to handle the request. The applicant can appear in person, or send the file by email or registered post. The consulate must reply within ten days of the request and must complete the visa process in one stop of the applicant, apart from the general documents defined in the decision under article 176, paragraph 8. This page does not restate that general list. Article 176(8) was not the text read here, so a police certificate, insurance wording, or a lease should be taken from the consulate's own current checklist, not inferred from article 68.

The title is a national visa. It is not, on the face of the sentences quoted here, a Schengen short-stay sticker and it is not a work permit for the Greek labour market. The whole structure of the article is that the work relationship sits outside Greece and the stay in Greece is the place from which that outside work is done.

Who can apply

Three working patterns fit the article, and they are written as alternatives, not as a stack.

An employee, or a person providing services or a defined project, shows a contract or other proof of the working relationship with an employer that is a natural or legal person established outside Greek territory. The contract is open-ended, or, if it is for a fixed term, the remaining term covers the period of the national visa. The file also identifies the applicant's status in the business and gives the business's name, seat, field of activity, and corporate purpose.

A freelancer with more than one employer shows contracts with those employers, each of them established outside Greece, on the same open-ended or remaining-term rule.

A person who is self-employed in their own business shows their status in that business and the business's name, seat, field, and purpose. The business itself is outside Greek territory.

Alongside the contracts, the applicant signs a declaration. It states the intention to reside in the country on the national visa in order to work remotely, and a commitment not to provide, in any way, work or services or a project to an employer whose seat is in Greece. That sentence is the line American applicants most often want to soften. A Greek client, a Greek subsidiary that is the employer of record, or a local contract "on the side" is the thing the declaration rules out. The article does not create an exception for a small Greek engagement.

The €3,500 resources test

Article 68 requires proof of sufficient resources, at the level of stable income, to cover living costs for the stay without burdening the national social-welfare system. It then sets the amount: three thousand five hundred euros a month. The proof is the employment or project contract, or the proof of the working relationship, where the income is dependent work, services, or a project; or a bank account. If the resources come from dependent work, services, or a project, the minimum is net income after the taxes required in the country where the work is provided. The amount is increased by twenty percent for the spouse or the cohabiting partner and by fifteen percent for each child.

A later instrument repeats the number and widens how it is read. Joint ministerial decision 225679 of the Ministers of National Economy and Finance, of Labour and Social Security, and of Migration and Asylum was signed on 4 September 2024 and published in FEK B 5223 on 17 September 2024. Article 1(1)(i) sets a minimum of €3,500 a month for the national visa and residence permit under article 163(8)(a) and (b) — the financially independent categories — increased by 20% for the spouse and 15% for each child. The next sentences of that point say that where the applicant asks either for the national visa of article 68(1) or for a residence permit under article 163(8) as a digital nomad, the resources are assessed on the applicant's income, monthly or yearly, applying the criteria in article 68(2)(b), (c), and (d). Those three points are the contract and business-identity rules above, not a different euro amount. Article 1(2) of the decision says the resources in paragraph 1 are net income or capital, subject to the particular rules of each category.

Read together, the code and the decision support one planning figure. Use €3,500 net a month for one applicant. Add 20% of that base for a spouse or cohabiting partner, and 15% of that base for each child. Twenty percent of €3,500 is €700, so a couple's combined floor is €4,200. Fifteen percent of €3,500 is €525 a child. A couple with one child is €4,725. A couple with two children is €5,250. The decision allows the assessment to be monthly, where the income is periodic, or annual, where it comes from other sources. It does not authorise a one-off savings balance to replace the €3,500 floor for a digital nomad unless that balance is the bank-account proof the code already allows, read as net resources. This page does not convert the euro floor into dollars. The decision states no exchange rate.

What the file must show

Put the declaration, the contract or business particulars, and the resources proof in one place, because the article lists them as what the applicant produces in addition to the general documents. For an American employee of a US company, the contract point is usually the employment agreement plus evidence of the employer's seat outside Greece. For a freelancer, it is the set of contracts whose remaining term covers the visa. For an owner of a non-Greek company, it is the company particulars the article names: status, name, seat, field, and purpose.

The resources proof follows the income. Dependent work is shown from the contract, and the €3,500 is a net figure after tax in the country of the work. A bank account is the other proof the article names. The September 2024 decision adds that the digital-nomad assessment can be monthly or annual and that the resources in its paragraph 1 are net. It does not publish a model bank statement or a required average-balance period, so this page does not invent one.

The national-visa fee in article 68 is seventy-five euros. That is the only fee the article states. It does not itemise a card fee, a biometric fee, or a dependent's fee. If a consulate collects something else, that charge is outside the sentence read here.

The ten-day reply is a statutory instruction to the consulate, measured from the applicant's request. It is not a promise that a US consulate's appointment calendar has a slot inside ten days, and it is not a processing time for a later residence card. Treat it as the response clock the code writes down, and get the appointment rules from the consulate that actually takes the file.

Spouse, partner, and children

The persons article 68 has already described may be accompanied by members of their family. Those members, on their own application, receive an individual visa that expires at the same time as the sponsor's visa. The income arithmetic above is how the code pays for that accompaniment: 20% for the spouse or cohabiting partner, 15% for each child, on top of the sponsor's €3,500.

The work ban on the family is broader than the sponsor's declaration. During the stay in Greece, the spouse or cohabiting partner and the family members of the sponsor are not allowed to provide dependent work or to exercise any form of economic activity in the country. A trailing spouse who intends to consult for Greek clients, or to take a local employment contract, is outside what this visa grants. A different residence category would have to be examined on its own text. This page does not recast the family visa as a work permit.

Twelve months, and what comes next

The grant in the opening of article 68 is a national entry visa of up to twelve months. That is the duration this page will state, because that is the duration the paragraph states.

The September 2024 decision is evidence that the system also has a residence-permit step. It expressly covers a person who asks for a residence permit under article 163(8) as a digital nomad, and it tells the administration to assess that person's resources with the article 68 contract criteria. A ministry circular index, in the file name used on migration.gov.gr, likewise refers to competence for granting the digital-nomad residence permit under article 68, paragraph 4. The body of paragraph 4 was not in the extract read for this guide, and the joint decision does not print a number of years for that card. So this page does not say the follow-on permit is two years, or three, or renewable on any particular cycle. Ask the office that issues the card, with article 68(4) and article 163(8) in front of you. Inventing the term would be worse than leaving it blank.

The same caution applies to physical presence. Article 68, as read here, does not impose a minimum number of days in Greece to keep the national visa. It also does not say that time in Greece is irrelevant to tax. Those are different statutes. The visa rule and the tax-residence rule can both be true.

Digital nomad, FIP, and Golden Visa

The three Greek routes Americans actually compare are easy to mix up because two of them now share a euro figure. They do not share a purpose.

Use the digital nomad article if the income is a job or a practice you will keep doing, for people outside Greece, while you live in Greece. Use the FIP visa if the income is a pension or other passive means and you will not work. Use the Golden Visa if the plan is the property itself and you do not need the stay in order to earn. Buying a home is a separate question from the visa. The guide to buying property in Greece is the place for transfer tax, which the finance ministry's capital-tax guide states at 3% of taxable value under Law 5219/2025, and for the annual property tax. Nothing in article 68 requires a purchase.

Greek tax if you stay

The Ministry of Economy and Finance income-tax guide, drawing on Law 4172/2013, states the resident and non-resident line in these words. A person who is a tax resident of Greece is taxed on taxable income arising in Greece and abroad. A person who is not a tax resident of Greece is taxed on taxable income arising in Greece. The same guide, on the page read for this article, does not restate a day-count. Do not treat "183 days" as a figure this page has re-verified. Confirm the residence test in article 4 of the Income Tax Code before you count nights.

If you do become a Greek tax resident, three special regimes exist, and only one of them is even in the neighbourhood of earned income. AADE's guide of 12 November 2025 describes Article 5A as a flat €100,000 a year on foreign-source income for 15 tax years, for a person who was not a Greek tax resident in seven of the previous eight years and who invests at least €500,000, with a waiver of that investment for a holder of an investment residence permit under article 16 of the older Law 4251/2014. Article 5B is 7% on all income arising abroad, for a person who receives a foreign pension, was not a Greek tax resident in five of the prior six years, and moves from a country with an administrative-cooperation agreement. Article 5C is described, in that guide's introduction, as a special method of taxation of income from employment and business activity arising in Greece.

Article 5C is the one people hope will cut the tax on a remote salary. The AADE description ties it to income arising in Greece from employment or business. Article 68 requires the employer, the clients, or the applicant's own business to be outside Greece, and it requires a declaration that the applicant will not provide work to an employer established in Greece. This page therefore does not apply Article 5C to the remote income the visa is built on. If a later ruling says otherwise, it will have to say so on an AADE or finance-ministry page. The November 2025 guide does not.

A non-resident who only has Greek-source income is in the other half of the ministry's sentence: Greece taxes that Greek-source income. Rent from a Greek property is Greek-source income from immovable property. From tax year 2026 the ministry's scale for that income is 15% up to €12,000, 25% from €12,001 to €24,000, 35% from €24,000.01 to €36,000, and 45% above €36,000. Owning the home is optional. If you do own one, the rental scale is the one to use, and the Golden Visa short-let limits are a different statute that applies to Golden Visa property, not automatically to every apartment a digital nomad rents as a tenant.

US tax that does not switch off

The IRS page for US citizens and resident aliens abroad states the rule that matters more than the visa. The rules for filing income, estate, and gift tax returns and for paying estimated tax are generally the same whether you are in the United States or abroad. You are taxed on worldwide income from all sources and you report that income under the Internal Revenue Code. The same page says that benefits such as the foreign earned income exclusion and the foreign tax credit are available only by filing a US return. It points the detail to Publication 54. This guide does not decide whether a particular reader meets the tax-home or physical-presence tests for the exclusion. Those tests are facts about the year, not a feature of the Greek visa.

A Greek bank account is a foreign financial account. The IRS FBAR page, applying the Bank Secrecy Act, says a US person must file FinCEN Form 114 if they have a financial interest in, or signature or other authority over, at least one financial account outside the United States and the aggregate value of those foreign financial accounts exceeded $10,000 at any time during the calendar year. The report is due 15 April, with an automatic extension to 15 October if you miss that date. You do not file the FBAR with the income-tax return. You file it electronically through FinCEN's BSA E-Filing System. Whether the account paid interest does not decide whether it is a foreign financial account. The more detailed treatment of when a property owner has to file is on the site's FBAR guide for foreign property owners. The foreign tax credit, if Greek tax is actually paid on income the United States also taxes, is claimed on a US return; the mechanics are on the foreign tax credit guide.

Two negative points are worth keeping. The Greek visa does not end US citizenship taxation. And a house, by itself, is not what the FBAR page describes. The FBAR page is about financial accounts. Opening the account that receives the salary, or that pays the rent, is the step that turns the move into an FBAR question. Do that inventory in the year you open the account, not the year after a notice.

A practical order of work

Start with the contract, not with a flight. Match your facts to one of the three patterns in article 68: one employer outside Greece, more than one client outside Greece, or your own business outside Greece. If the contract is fixed-term, check that the remaining term covers the visa you will ask for. Then compute the net monthly figure the way the article does, including 20% and 15% if a partner or children will hold their own visas, and line up either the contract evidence or the bank-account evidence.

Write the declaration in the words the article uses: remote work, and no work, services, or project for an employer established in Greece. Budget the €75 national-visa fee the article names, and ask the consulate for the general documents under article 176(8) rather than copying a blog's list. File so that the ten-day response clock has a request to run from. If the plan is to stay past the national visa, ask in writing how article 68(4) or article 163(8) is being applied to digital nomads, and do not assume a card length this page refused to invent.

Separately, before the first full calendar year in Greece, have a US preparer and a Greek accountant say whether that year makes you a Greek tax resident under article 4, and whether any Greek tax will be creditable. The visa decision and the tax-residence decision are not the same filing. The Greece hub collects the property and Golden Visa pages if the stay turns into a purchase. The digital nomad visa does not require that purchase, and it does not replace the US return.

US Estate Tax on Foreign Property (2026)

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated November 2026  ·  14 min read
A US citizen's foreign house is inside the federal gross estate. The July 2026 Instructions for Form 706 say the gross estate includes property outside the United States. For a death in 2026 the basic exclusion amount is $15,000,000 and the basic credit amount is $5,945,800. Table A puts a 40% rate on a taxable amount over $1,000,000. The return and the tax are due nine months after death. A foreign inheritance tax does not replace that return. It can be a credit, on Schedule P with Form 706-CE, only up to the US estate tax attributable to the property, and only once it has actually been paid.

Who files, and on which form

The July 2026 instructions split the world in one paragraph. File Form 706 for a decedent who was a US citizen or a US resident at death. For this tax, a resident is a person domiciled in the United States. The instructions say a person acquires domicile by living in a place, even for a short time, with no definite present intention of moving later, and they point to Regulations section 20.0-1(b). A person who was neither a citizen nor a resident files Form 706-NA.

Citizenship is the fact that keeps a house in Lisbon, Mérida, or Penang on Form 706. Moving abroad, renting out the US house, or spending the year in the foreign one does not, by itself, change the form. The gross-estate sentence is explicit: it includes all property in which the decedent had an interest, including property outside the United States. It also includes certain lifetime transfers, annuities, the includible part of joint interests and tenancies by the entirety, certain life insurance, property subject to a general power of appointment, dower or curtesy, and community property to the extent of the decedent's interest as defined by the law that applies. Those extra categories are why a deed in one name is not the whole return.

For a citizen or resident who died in 2026, Form 706 is required in two cases. The first is a gross estate, plus adjusted taxable gifts and the specific exemption, of more than $15,000,000. Adjusted taxable gifts are those after 31 December 1976. The specific exemption is the old section 2521 amount for gifts after 8 September 1976 and before the 1976 repeal. The second case is an election to transfer the deceased spousal unused exclusion to the surviving spouse, whatever the size of the estate. An estate under the threshold that skips the return also skips portability, unless it later qualifies for the relief described below.

The exclusion and the credit are the same shield, read two ways. Table A puts a tax of $345,800 on the first $1,000,000 and 40% on the rest. Tax on a $15,000,000 taxable estate is $345,800 plus 40% of $14,000,000, which is $5,600,000. The sum is $5,945,800, the published basic credit. Subtract the credit and the estate tax is zero, if there are no adjusted taxable gifts and no other adjustment. A taxable estate of $16,000,000 produces a tentative tax of $6,345,800. After the same credit, $400,000 remains, which is 40% of the $1,000,000 above the exclusion. That $400,000 is before the foreign death-tax credit. It is the arithmetic of the published table, not a bill for a particular house.

Schedule A and the mortgage

If the gross estate contains any real estate, the instructions say to complete Schedule A and file it with the return. List real estate the decedent owned or had contracted to purchase. Describe it so the IRS can locate it: area, improvements, and for a city property the street and number. Explain how the value was determined and attach appraisals. The value is not reduced for a homestead exemption or for dower, curtesy, or a statutory estate in place of them.

A mortgage is not a percentage the instructions leave to the reader. If the estate is liable for the mortgage — the debt can be charged against other property that is not subject to that mortgage — or the decedent was personally liable, report the full value in the date-of-death column and enter the mortgage in the description. The unpaid amount may be deducted on Schedule K. If the estate is not liable, report only the equity of redemption, which is the value less the debt, and do not enter less than zero. Do not deduct that debt on Schedule K. A house the decedent had contracted to buy is reported at full value, and the unpaid price is deducted on Schedule K.

Alternate valuation under section 2032 is an election for the whole estate, not for the foreign house alone. The instructions allow it only when it decreases both the value of the gross estate and the estate and GST tax payable on the property included. Value the property six months after death, or on the date it was distributed, sold, or otherwise disposed of if that came first. The election, once made, may not be revoked. It may be made on a late Form 706 if the return is not filed later than one year after the due date, including extensions actually granted.

The return is due nine months after death. The tax is due then as well. Form 4768 requests an automatic six-month extension of time to file. An extension of time to pay is a separate request on that form. The instructions also point to section 6166 installment payment and section 6163 postponement for a reversion or remainder, each made by checking the relevant line and attaching the statement the form requires. This page does not restate who qualifies for section 6166.

Portability has its own clock. The executor can transfer the unused exclusion only if Form 706 is filed within nine months, or within the six-month filing extension. If the estate had no filing requirement under section 6018(a) and missed that window, Rev. Proc. 2022-32 lets the executor file on or before the fifth anniversary of the death. The top of that return has to say it is filed pursuant to Rev. Proc. 2022-32 to elect portability under section 2010(c)(5)(A).

The foreign house and the US return are one estate.

The foreign death-tax credit

A local inheritance or estate tax on the same house is not automatically a US credit, and it is not a reason to skip Form 706. The July 2026 instructions put the credit on Schedule P. The total from Schedule P, line 11, goes to Form 706, Part II, line 13, and Form 706-CE is attached for each foreign death tax. If the foreign government refuses to certify Form 706-CE, the instructions say to file it directly with the IRS as the form itself directs.

The same instructions limit the credit. For any property, whether one foreign country taxed it or more than one, the total credit cannot exceed the federal estate tax attributable to that property. The anticipated credit may be shown on the return. It cannot finally be allowed until the foreign tax has been paid and Form 706-CE evidencing payment is filed. Section 2014(g), as the instructions recite it, treats each US territory as a foreign country for this credit.

IRM 4.25.4.5 adds the claim window and two limits that the form instructions do not spell out in the paragraph above. The credit is limited to tax actually paid and claimed within the later of four years after the Form 706 filing date, the end of any extension of time to pay the federal estate tax, or 60 days after a final Tax Court decision on a timely petition. If the decedent was a US resident and a citizen of another country, the credit is allowed only to the extent that country of citizenship allows a corresponding credit for US citizens who resided there. The manual also says a taxpayer takes either the section 2014 credit or a deduction for the foreign death tax under section 2053(d), not both. If a credited foreign tax is later recovered, 26 CFR 20.2016-1 requires notice to the IRS.

The practical order is the opposite of the brochure version. Compute the US tax first. Then ask what foreign death tax was paid on property that was included in the US gross estate. Then cap the credit at the US tax attributable to that property. A country that charges no inheritance tax produces a credit of zero. The local rules on who inherits, including forced heirship, are a different statute. They are covered in inheritance tax on property abroad.

Estate-tax treaties

An income-tax treaty does not do this work. The IRS estate-and-gift treaty page is a separate list. IRM 4.25.4 says each estate-tax treaty is unique, that many of them leave the country where real property sits with a taxing right over that property, and that the United States generally retains the right to tax its citizens and residents under the treaty. A treaty is a credit and a situs document. It is not a switch that takes a citizen's foreign house off Form 706.

A spouse who is not a US citizen

The unlimited marital deduction is the rule readers import from a US will. IRM 4.25.4 says a transfer to a surviving spouse who is not a US citizen does not follow that rule. The deduction is available if the property passes to a qualified domestic trust under section 2056A. The property has to be transferred to the trust before the death or before the Form 706 is filed. The election is made on a Form 706 filed no more than one year after the due date, including extensions, and Schedule M lists the trust.

The manual then sorts trusts by size. If the value passing to the QDOT exceeds $2 million, the trust must meet at least one of the security conditions in the manual at all times. If the value is $2 million or less, a lighter set applies, including the option to meet the over-$2-million conditions. More than one QDOT is aggregated for the $2 million test. The executor may elect to exclude up to $600,000 of real property, including furnishings, from that value if the spouse uses it as a personal residence, the trust owns it directly, and the property passed to the trust under the marital-deduction rules. The trustee files Form 706-QDT when the trust has a taxable event. If the estate also elected portability, the manual says the unused-exclusion amount shown on Form 706 is preliminary and is finally determined when the spouse dies or the trust ends.

None of that is a reason to title the foreign house in the noncitizen spouse's name on the eve of death. A lifetime transfer has its own gift-tax rules, and this page does not restate them. The point for the estate return is narrower: the marital deduction for a noncitizen spouse is a trust election, with a deadline, not a line that fills itself in because the couple was married.

The heir's basis

Section 1014(a) sets the basis of property acquired from a decedent, or passed from a decedent, at its fair market value on the date of death. If the estate elects section 2032, the basis is the value at the alternate-valuation date that section prescribes. The subsection does not say "property situated in the United States." A foreign house the heir acquired from the decedent takes that basis.

Section 1014(f) is the cap. Basis cannot exceed the value finally determined for the estate tax, or the value reported to the heir under section 6035, but only for property whose inclusion increased the chapter 11 tax after credits. If the estate tax is zero with the house and zero without it, inclusion did not increase the tax, and that cap does not apply. The date-of-death fair market value is still the basis under subsection (a). The executor who will someday sell, or whose heir will, wants an appraisal attached to Schedule A anyway. The instructions require the value to be explained. A later sale is reported on the heir's income-tax return, which is the subject of Form 8949 for a foreign property sale, not of Form 706.

What the executor files

  1. Decide the form. A citizen, and a noncitizen who was domiciled in the United States, is Form 706 and the worldwide estate. Anyone else is Form 706-NA and US-situated assets only.
  2. List the foreign real estate on Schedule A if it belonged to a citizen or resident, with the mortgage rule above, and attach the appraisal.
  3. File and pay within nine months. Use Form 4768 if the return will be late. Ask separately if the tax payment needs more time. Put the portability legend on a fifth-anniversary filing that Rev. Proc. 2022-32 allows.
  4. Claim a foreign death tax only after it is paid, on Schedule P with Form 706-CE, and only up to the US estate tax attributable to that property.
  5. Read the treaty if the decedent's country is on the IRS estate-and-gift list. Do not treat an income-tax treaty as a substitute.
  6. If the spouse is not a US citizen, decide before the return whether the property will pass to a qualified domestic trust, and make the election on the Form 706.

If the owner was not a US person

Form 706-NA, in the September 2025 instructions, computes the estate tax of a decedent who was neither domiciled in nor a citizen of the United States. The executor files if the date-of-death value of US-situated assets, together with the gift-tax specific exemption and adjusted taxable gifts, exceeds $60,000. The return is due nine months after death unless an extension was granted. The unified credit entered on that return is the smaller of the tax computed on the form or the maximum unified credit, and in general that maximum is $13,000.

What counts as situated in the United States is not a guess from the deed's language. Regulations section 20.2104-1(a)(1) says real property of a nonresident who was not a citizen is situated in the United States if it is located in the United States. A house in another country fails that sentence. The Form 706-NA instructions tell the executor to list, on the US-assets schedule, only assets that are both in the gross estate and located in the United States, and to put assets located outside the United States on a different line. A nonresident's foreign house is not a US estate-tax asset. A nonresident's condominium in Miami is. Treaties on the list above can change the nonresident result. The instructions say to consult the treaty where one applies.

The rest of this site's inheritance page still matters for that owner: local forced heirship and local inheritance tax do not turn on the US form. They turn on the law of the place where the property sits, and on whether a will made the election that law allows.

Frequently asked questions

Is a foreign house included in a US citizen's estate?

Yes. The July 2026 Instructions for Form 706 say the gross estate includes all property in which the decedent had an interest, including property outside the United States. A US citizen files Form 706, not Form 706-NA. Living abroad does not remove the house or switch the citizen onto the nonresident return.

What is the US estate-tax exclusion for a death in 2026?

The July 2026 instructions set the basic exclusion amount at $15,000,000 and the basic credit amount at $5,945,800. Table A taxes the amount over $1,000,000 at 40%. On these figures, a taxable estate of $15,000,000 produces a tentative tax equal to the credit, so the estate tax is zero if there are no adjusted taxable gifts and no other adjustments. A taxable estate of $16,000,000 produces $400,000 of tax before any foreign death-tax credit.

How does a mortgage on the foreign home go on Form 706?

The Schedule A instructions draw a line. If the estate is liable for the mortgage, or the decedent was personally liable, report the full value and deduct the unpaid mortgage on Schedule K. If the estate is not liable, report only the equity, not below zero, and do not deduct the debt on Schedule K.

Can the estate credit a foreign inheritance tax against the US estate tax?

A credit for foreign death taxes is claimed on Schedule P, and Form 706-CE is attached. The instructions limit the credit for any property to the federal estate tax attributable to that property, and they say the credit cannot finally be allowed until the foreign tax has been paid and Form 706-CE is filed. IRM 4.25.4.5.4 limits the claim to the later of four years after the Form 706 filing date, the end of any extension of time to pay, or 60 days after a final Tax Court decision. The same tax is not both a credit and a deduction.

What if the surviving spouse is not a US citizen?

IRM 4.25.4 says a transfer to a surviving spouse who is not a US citizen does not receive the marital deduction unless the property passes to a qualified domestic trust under section 2056A. The property must reach the trust before death or before the Form 706 is filed. The election is made on a Form 706 filed no more than one year after the due date, including extensions. If more than $2 million passes to the trust, the manual's security conditions apply. Up to $600,000 of real property the spouse uses as a home can be excluded from that value test when the trust owns it directly.

Does the heir get a step-up in basis on the foreign home?

Section 1014(a) sets the basis of property acquired from a decedent at its fair market value on the date of death, or at the alternate-valuation value if the estate elects section 2032. The section does not limit that rule to property inside the United States. Section 1014(f) caps basis at the finally determined estate-tax value only when including the property increased the estate tax, after credits. If the estate tax is zero either way, that cap does not apply.

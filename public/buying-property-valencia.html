Buying Property in Valencia as an American (2026): Neighbourhoods, ITP, Tourist-Let Rules and Tax

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated October 2026  ·  12 min read
Americans can buy in Valencia city or on the Costa Blanca with the same title rights as a Spaniard. From 1 June 2026 a resale in the Valencian Community pays 9% ITP up to €1 million and 11% on the whole value once the price or reference value goes over that line. A new build pays 10% VAT plus 1.4% AJD. Tourist lets are a separate licence, not a feature of the deed, and a purchase still does not give you residency.

Valencia is two purchases that share one tax statute. The city — Ruzafa, El Carmen, Cabanyal, Benimaclet — is an urban apartment market with community rules and a live tourist-licence question on almost every listing. An hour south, Dénia and Jávea sit in Alicante province, on the same regional tax, with a different town hall and a different short-let map. The national process is in our guide to buying property in Spain: NIE, notary, land registry. This page is the Valencian layer — the rate that replaced the old flat 10%, the tourist-housing decree, and the 2026 court ruling that took apart Spain's national rental-registration number.

What the official appraisal series actually says

Spain's housing ministry publishes a quarterly valor tasado — the average appraisal value of homes valued by appraisal companies, in euros per square metre. It is not an asking price and not a deed price. In the second quarter of 2026 the free-market series read:

The series does not split Ruzafa from Cabanyal, or Dénia from the rest of Alicante. A listing well above these figures can still be a real asking price; it is not a government statistic. The number that drives your transfer tax is different again: the cadastral valor de referencia on the accrual date, or the price if the price is higher.

ITP, the €1 million cliff, and a €400,000 example

Until 31 May 2026 the general resale rate in this region was 10%, with 11% already applying above €1 million. Ley 5/2025, de 30 de mayo, article 33, rewrote article 13.1 of Ley 13/1997 for taxable events from 1 June 2026. The general rate is now 9% on acquisitions of real estate and on real rights over it, other than security interests. If the value is over €1 million, the rate on that transfer is 11%. The statute applies 11% to the transaction once the threshold is crossed. It is not a slice of tax only on the excess. The regional tax agency, the ATV, states the same rule and adds that related purchases of the same registered property from the same seller inside three years are treated as one transfer for the rate.

The tax base, again per the ATV, is the cadastral reference value. If the declared value or the agreed price is higher, the higher figure is the base. Where no reference value can be certified, the base is the higher of the declared value, the price, and market value, subject to an administrative check.

A new home bought from a developer is outside ITP. It carries 10% VAT on housing, and the notarial deed is charged to stamp duty. Article 34 of the same law set the general AJD rate at 1.4% from 1 June 2026, down from 1.5%. Reduced ITP rates still exist in later paragraphs of article 13 — including an 8% rate for some protected homes and some first habitual homes for buyers under 35 — but they require the home to be the buyer's actual residence and other conditions a non-resident vacation purchase will not meet. Budget the 9% or the 11%.

Cross the cliff and the bill jumps on the entire price. On a €1,100,000 resale the 11% rate is €121,000. The same price at 9% would have been €99,000. That €22,000 gap is why a reference value sitting just over €1 million has to be checked before you agree a number, not after the notary reads the deed.

Annual IBI is municipal. Valencia city, Dénia, and Jávea each set their own rate on the cadastral value in that year's tax ordinance. There is no regional IBI percentage to quote. Ask for the last receipt. Community fees are not a tax either: under the Horizontal Property Law the owners' association sets the budget, and a tourist use can be blocked in the bylaws even when the town hall would allow it.

Tourist lets after Decreto-ley 9/2024

Decreto-ley 9/2024, de 2 de agosto, redrew what counts as a vivienda de uso turístico in this region. It is a whole dwelling, any type, let ready for immediate use, for tourism, for a continuous stay of 10 days or less with the same guest, and it needs a favourable municipal urban-compatibility report (or the equivalent the regulation allows). The dwelling is let whole. Letting by the room is prohibited, and doing so in a dwelling that is already on the tourism register is classed as a very serious infringement. A stay of 11 days or more with the same guest is not this tourist category. It falls under the ordinary urban-leases law instead.

Registration in the regional tourism register lasts five years. When that term ends without a renewal declaration and, where required, a new municipal report, the dwelling is removed from the register. The responsible declaration has to state a unique cadastral reference, an occupancy title, civil-liability cover, and a registry certificate that the community's title or bylaws — or a resolution opposable to third parties — do not ban a use other than a habitual home. Advertising has to show the exact location and the regional register number. That is a serious infringement if you skip it.

Separately, Real Decreto 1312/2024 built a national single-registration procedure and a digital one-stop shop for short stays. The decree took effect on 2 January 2025 and its operative provisions from 1 July 2025. On 19 May 2026 the Supreme Court annulled the parts that created that unique registration procedure — including articles 8, 9 and 10 — and dismissed the rest of the challenge. The judgment is in the BOE of 8 June 2026. The Valencian tourist-housing rules above were not the subject of that ruling. Before you underwrite a listing, have a lawyer say which number a platform is still allowed to demand, and whether this specific building can get the municipal report at all.

Possession, community rules, and the nota simple

Two checks matter more in this city than a glossy floor plan. First, the nota simple from the land registry: owner, charges, and the property description, matched to the cadastral reference on the contract. Second, who is in the property on the day of completion. A paying tenant on an ordinary lease is a contract problem with a notice period. A person in occupation with no title is a possession problem, and the notary's deed does not by itself put them out. Walk the property, or have your lawyer's local contact do it, before the balance moves. Do not treat a seller's assurance that "it will be empty" as a substitute for seeing it empty.

In an apartment, read the community statutes for a tourist-use ban and for special assessments. The tourist-licence declaration itself asks for registry proof that no such ban is opposable. A building that has already voted one down is not a short-let investment, whatever the portal says.

There is no statute that sets a non-resident mortgage percentage. Spanish banks decide loan size case by case, and many will not lend to a buyer who lives in the United States. Treat any loan-to-value a broker quotes as that bank's policy. Our guide to foreign mortgages for US citizens covers how to compare a Spanish offer with paying cash or borrowing at home.

Buying is not a visa

Spain's investor residence, including the real-estate route, was emptied out of Ley 14/2013 by Ley Orgánica 1/2025, with effect from 3 April 2025. Owning in Ruzafa or Jávea does not give you a residence card. The route most Americans use to live here is the non-lucrative visa. Consular pages of the foreign ministry state the 2026 means test as €2,400 a month for the applicant and €600 a month for each family member, equal to 400% and 100% of the IPREM of €600. The visa does not allow work. The digital-nomad visa is the working alternative, and it has its own income test. Neither one is issued because you bought a home.

Step-by-step

  1. Get a NIE and appoint an independent lawyer. The seller's agent is not your lawyer.
  2. Pull the nota simple and the cadastral reference value before you agree a price. If either the price or the reference value is over €1 million, price the 11% rate, not 9%.
  3. If you want short-let income, get the municipal compatibility position and the community-statute certificate before you rely on it. Read the five-year register rule.
  4. Sign the reservation or arras contract only after those checks. A deposit is hard to unwind.
  5. See the property vacant, or know exactly which lawful occupant stays, before completion.
  6. Complete at the notary. On a resale the buyer pays ITP, generally within 30 days, on the ATV's form. On a new build the developer charges VAT and the deed carries AJD.
  7. Register the deed and, if you will live here, start the visa on its own track. Open the Spanish account you will use for IBI and community fees with FBAR in mind.

The US tax that still applies

A Spanish purchase does not change US citizenship taxation. Rent from a Valencia flat goes on Schedule E. A sale is a US capital gain. Spanish tax on the same income is the usual candidate for a foreign tax credit, subject to the limits in the instructions for Form 1116. An empty home is the awkward case: Spain imputes income on an urban property you do not let, at 1.1% of the cadastral value where a collective valuation took effect in the tax year or the ten before it, and 2% otherwise, then taxes that amount at the general non-resident rate. For a resident of the United States that general rate is 24%, against 19% for residents of the EU, Iceland, Norway, and Liechtenstein. There is often no US income in that year to credit the Spanish bill against.

A gain on the sale is also declared on Modelo 210. The rate for a real-estate gain is the one article 25 of the non-resident income-tax law assigns to that category. Do not assume it equals the 24% rate on rent and imputed income. Read the current Modelo 210 instructions, or have the Spanish accountant do it, before you model the credit.

A Spanish account used for community fees or rent is a foreign account for FBAR purposes if the aggregate of foreign accounts crosses the threshold, and it can also be a FATCA asset. The property itself is not a bank account. The account you open to run it is. More of the US filing picture is in US tax on foreign property.

If you're not American

The Valencian rates and the tourist-housing decree apply to every foreign buyer. What changes with your passport is the tax at home, and the Spanish non-resident rate: EU and EEA residents pay 19% on imputed income and on rent, not the 24% that applies to a US resident.

If you do not file a US return, drop the FBAR section and substitute your own country's rules on foreign rent, foreign gains, and foreign accounts. The 9% and 11% ITP rates, the 1.4% AJD, the five-year tourism register, and the ban on room lets do not change.

Is Valencia the right Spanish buy in 2026?

It is a strong fit if you want a city with a real year-round life, or a Costa Blanca town, and you will budget 9% transfer tax without pretending a listing's nightly rate is already licensed. It is a weak fit if the plan only works as a room-by-room tourist let, if the reference value is about to tip the purchase over €1 million, or if you were counting on the deed to produce a residence card. Read the Costa del Sol guide if you want Andalucía's flat 7% instead, and the note on the closed investor visa if a broker is still selling residency with the apartment.

Frequently asked questions

Can an American buy property in Valencia?

Yes. Spain does not restrict foreign ownership, so a US citizen can buy an apartment or house in Valencia city or on the Costa Blanca in their own name, without residency. You need a NIE, the foreigner identification number, and an independent Spanish lawyer. Buying does not by itself give you the right to live in Spain.

What transfer tax do I pay in the Valencian Community in 2026?

On a resale home, from 1 June 2026 the general ITP rate is 9% when the value is not over €1 million, and 11% on the whole value once it is over €1 million, under article 33 of Ley 5/2025 amending Ley 13/1997. The tax base is the higher of the cadastral reference value, the declared value, and the price. A new build bought from a developer pays 10% VAT instead of ITP, plus 1.4% AJD stamp duty on the notarial deed.

Can I rent a Valencia apartment to tourists?

Only as a whole dwelling registered as a vivienda de uso turístico. Decreto-ley 9/2024 defines that use as a stay of 10 days or less with the same guest, bans renting by the room, requires a favourable municipal compatibility report, and limits a regional tourism-register entry to five years. Advertising a room-by-room tourist let in a registered dwelling is a very serious infringement. The national single-registration procedure in Real Decreto 1312/2024 was annulled in part by a Supreme Court judgment of 19 May 2026, so confirm with a lawyer what a platform still requires before you count on short-let income.

Does buying in Valencia give me Spanish residency?

No. Spain emptied the investor-residence articles of Ley 14/2013 with effect from 3 April 2025. Americans who want to live in Valencia apply separately, most often for the non-lucrative visa. In 2026 the foreign ministry's consular pages set that income test at €2,400 a month for the main applicant and €600 a month for each dependent, which is 400% and 100% of the IPREM.

What happened to Spain's national short-term rental registry?

Real Decreto 1312/2024 created a single rental-registration procedure and a digital one-stop shop, with effects from 1 July 2025. A Supreme Court judgment of 19 May 2026, published in the BOE on 8 June 2026, annulled the provisions that set up that unique registration procedure, including articles 8, 9 and 10, and left the rest of the decree standing. Regional tourist-housing rules in the Valencian Community were not struck down by that judgment.

Do I owe US tax on a home in Valencia?

Yes if you are a US citizen: rental income and a later sale are reportable in the United States even though Spain also taxes Spanish-source property income. An empty home can still produce Spanish imputed income, taxed at 24% for a US resident on 1.1% or 2% of the cadastral value, with no matching US income to credit. A Spanish bank account can trigger FBAR and FATCA reporting.

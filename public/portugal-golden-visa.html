Portugal Golden Visa 2026: Cost, Requirements & US Citizen Guide

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated September 2026  ·  14 min read
Yes — Americans can still get a Portugal Golden Visa in 2026, but not by buying property. The real-estate route was removed in 2023. The main path today is a €500,000 investment in a qualifying CMVM-regulated fund (or €250,000 to support arts and culture). It requires only ~7 days a year in Portugal, grants Schengen access, and — under Lei Orgânica 1/2026 — leads to citizenship eligibility after 10 years (7 for EU/CPLP nationals), with a permanent-residence alternative available at year 5.

For Americans wanting a European "plan B" — Schengen access, an EU residency, and a path to a second passport — Portugal's Golden Visa has been the most popular option in the world. US citizens are now the single largest group of applicants. But the program in 2026 looks very different from the one most online guides still describe: the real-estate route is gone, the fund route has real fine print, processing is slower than advertised, and the citizenship clock just got longer. Here's what actually applies today, with the numbers and the traps most guides skip.

What is the Portugal Golden Visa?

The Golden Visa — formally the Autorização de Residência para Investimento (ARI), created under Article 90.º-A of Lei n.º 23/2007 (the Foreigners' Law) — is a residency-by-investment program. In exchange for a qualifying investment, you and your family receive Portuguese residency permits, the right to live, work and study in Portugal, and visa-free travel across the Schengen Area. Crucially, you are not required to relocate — the physical-presence requirement is only an average of about 7 days per year (14 days across each two-year permit), making it a true backup plan rather than a forced move.

Why Americans choose it

  • EU access without moving: Schengen travel and an EU base, while you keep living in the US.
  • Family included: spouse, dependent children, and dependent parents can be added to one application.
  • Low stay requirement: ~7 days a year, far lighter than most residency programs.
  • Path to citizenship: an EU passport at the end of the road — now a longer road than it used to be (see below).

2026 investment routes, legal basis, and government fees

Since the property route was removed, every qualifying option under Article 90.º-A is capital-based. The four routes actually in use:

Fund due diligence: what to check before you wire €500,000

The fund route is now the default for most American investors because it is passive and clearly documented — but "CMVM-regulated" is a floor, not a guarantee of quality, and the fine print determines whether your capital comes back.

  • Confirm CMVM registration directly. Every qualifying fund must be registered with the Comissão do Mercado de Valores Mobiliários (CMVM), Portugal's securities regulator. Check the fund's registration on CMVM's own public register rather than taking a placement agent's word for it, and confirm the fund manager is also CMVM-authorized.
  • Understand the fee drag. Typical structures charge a one-time subscription or setup fee (roughly 0%–3%, sometimes deducted from the invested capital rather than added on top), an annual management fee (roughly 1%–2.5%), and a performance fee (commonly 20%–25% above a hurdle rate of 5%–8%). On a €500,000 ticket held for 6–10 years, these fees compound meaningfully against your net return.
  • Read the lock-up and redemption terms. Private equity and venture-capital Golden Visa funds typically run 6–10 year lifecycles. Because the law requires you to maintain the qualifying investment throughout your residency-to-citizenship period, an early redemption can jeopardize your visa status, not just your return.
  • Distrust "capital guarantee" marketing. A fund investment is an equity or debt stake in real assets and companies — it carries genuine investment risk. Any pitch that promises a guaranteed return alongside Golden Visa eligibility deserves the same skepticism you'd apply to that promise anywhere else.
  • No real estate, directly or indirectly. Since October 2023, a fund that holds property — even a small allocation, even through a holding company — no longer qualifies. Ask the fund manager for the underlying portfolio, not just a compliance letter.
⚠️ The US tax trap almost no fund brochure mentions: PFIC status. A Portuguese Golden Visa fund is, for US tax purposes, almost always a Passive Foreign Investment Company (PFIC) — its income comes overwhelmingly from dividends, interest, capital gains and rents rather than an active trade or business. That triggers annual Form 8621 filing, and the default "excess distribution" regime that applies if you do nothing can push your effective tax rate on eventual gains above 50% through top-rate tax plus an interest charge computed back across your entire holding period. A QEF (Qualified Electing Fund) election — taxing your pro-rata share of the fund's income annually at ordinary rates, whether or not it's distributed — is usually the better outcome, but it requires the fund to furnish an annual PFIC information statement, and not every Golden Visa fund manager will. The alternative mark-to-market election requires "marketable stock," a test most private Golden Visa fund units fail. Ask the fund manager, in writing, whether they will provide a PFIC/QEF annual statement before you subscribe — and get a US cross-border CPA to model both election paths against the default regime first.

Why you can no longer buy property for it

This is the single biggest misconception. Since October 2023, real-estate investment no longer qualifies for the Portugal Golden Visa. The government removed the property route to ease housing-affordability pressure on locals. Any 2026 guide still telling you to "buy a €500K apartment for your Golden Visa" is out of date. If your goal is to own a home in Portugal, you still can — see our Portugal property guide — but that is a separate decision from the visa, and it won't earn you residency.

The real 2026 processing timeline — and the backlog

Marketing materials still quote the statutory processing targets; the lived experience in 2026 is slower. A realistic path, stage by stage:

AIMA (the successor agency to the old SEF, formed in the 2023 reorganization) has been working through a substantial backlog of both new ARI applications and citizenship files. As of 2026, applicants commonly report 6 to 24 months from filing to a biometrics appointment, and a further 6 to 18 months from biometrics to physical card issuance — meaning a realistic end-to-end total runs from roughly a year on the fast end to well over three years for applicants caught in the earlier backlog. This is not a minor administrative delay: because the naturalization clock now starts from the date your first residence permit is issued (not from your application or investment date — see below), a long AIMA queue directly delays your citizenship eligibility, on top of the years the citizenship rule itself now requires. A consortium of law firms filed an ombudsman complaint on behalf of over a thousand affected applicants during 2026, and a separate group of Golden Visa holders has pursued litigation specifically over the lack of transitional protection in the 2026 nationality-law changes discussed next. Budget for the slow case, not the brochure case, and ask your lawyer for their office's current, office-specific wait times rather than a national average.

Citizenship under Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026: the real timeline

Historically, Golden Visa holders could apply for Portuguese citizenship after just 5 years of holding a residence permit — one of the program's biggest draws. Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026, which took effect 19 May 2026, rewrote that timeline and added new substantive requirements:

  • 10 years of legal residence for most non-EU nationals, including Americans (up from 5) — or 7 years for nationals of EU/EEA countries and CPLP (Portuguese-speaking) countries.
  • The clock now runs from the date your first residence permit was issued by AIMA — not your application date, and not your investment date. Given the AIMA backlogs above, a slow first-card issuance pushes your effective citizenship-eligibility date back further still.
  • The existing A2-level Portuguese language requirement continues to apply, and the reform layers on a civic-knowledge test covering Portuguese culture, history and political organization (the detailed implementing rules for this test were still pending as of mid-2026), plus a formal declaration of adherence to democratic rule-of-law principles.
  • Clean-record standard: no convictions carrying a sentence over 3 years, with heightened scrutiny for terrorism, organized crime or state-security offenses, and no active UN or EU restrictive measures against the applicant.

Transitional rule. Citizenship applications submitted to the IRN (Instituto dos Registos e do Notariado) on or before 18 May 2026 continue to be processed under the prior 5-year regime (Lei n.º 37/81). Applicants who had already accumulated 5+ years of residence but had not yet filed by the cutoff received no grandfathering into the old rule — a gap that triggered the collective legal challenges mentioned above. Portugal's Constitutional Court ruled on parts of the reform in December 2025: it struck down automatic nationality revocation for serious post-naturalization crimes and protected already-pending applications from retroactive treatment, but upheld the core extension of the naturalization timeline and the new start-date rule. If your first residence card predates May 2026 and you were approaching the old 5-year mark, get a same-week read from your immigration lawyer on whether any transitional relief still applies to your specific filing date.

The permanent-residence alternative most guides skip. You do not have to wait for citizenship to get most of its practical benefits. Permanent residence at 5 years remains available under the unchanged general Aliens Act regime (Art. 80.º of Lei 23/2007) and was not touched by the 2026 nationality reform. It gives you an indefinite right to live, work and access services in Portugal on the same footing as a citizen for everyday purposes — everything except the EU passport itself, the unrestricted EU-wide right to vote, and visa-free access to non-Schengen countries that specifically require citizenship. For an American whose real goal is the Schengen base and the option to relocate, rather than a second passport per se, permanent residence at year 5 is worth planning around explicitly, in parallel with — not instead of — the longer citizenship track.

Family reunification: who else gets covered

A single Golden Visa application can extend to your immediate family, each receiving their own residence card with identical rights to live, work and study in Portugal, and following the same path to permanent residence and citizenship:

  • Spouse or de-facto partner — married or in a legally recognized civil union.
  • Minor children, including adopted children, under 18.
  • Adult children up to roughly age 26, while unmarried, financially dependent, and in full-time education.
  • Parents (yours or your spouse's) — typically over 65, or younger where they are genuinely financially dependent on you.

The qualifying investment itself is made only once, by the main applicant — you don't multiply the €500,000 by family size. What does multiply is the AIMA government fee schedule above (analysis, issuance and renewal fees are charged per person) and your lawyer's per-dependent filing costs, so a family of four should budget those fees four times over, not once.

US & Portuguese tax impact

Holding a Golden Visa does not, by itself, make you a Portuguese tax resident. You generally become tax resident only if you spend more than 183 days a year in Portugal or establish it as your habitual home. Because the program requires only ~7 days a year, most American holders never trigger Portuguese tax residency.

As a US citizen, however, you remain subject to US tax on worldwide income no matter where you live or invest. The PFIC exposure on the fund route covered above is the sharpest edge here — model it with a cross-border CPA before you invest, not after your first Form 8621 is due. If instead you eventually do move to Portugal and become tax resident, note that the old NHR regime closed to new entrants on 1 January 2025 and was replaced by the narrower IFICI ("NHR 2.0"), limited to qualifying professionals in research, science, technology and certain high-value sectors.

Golden Visa vs. D7 vs. D8: which fits you?

The Golden Visa is not the only door into Portugal, and for many Americans it isn't the cheapest one. If you're willing to actually live in Portugal — rather than keep the US as your base and Portugal as a backup — the D7 or D8 visas get you the same eventual permanent-residence and citizenship track for a fraction of the capital outlay:

In short: the Golden Visa buys optionality without relocation, at a six-figure entry cost; the D7 and D8 buy the same eventual passport for a low four-figure annual income requirement, at the cost of actually moving. See our full Portugal D7 visa guide for the passive-income route in detail.

Step-by-step: applying as an American

  1. Get a Portuguese NIF (tax number) and open a Portuguese bank account.
  2. Choose your route — most pick a qualifying €500K fund; run CMVM and PFIC due diligence first (above).
  3. Run US tax due diligence on the specific fund (PFIC exposure, QEF-statement availability) with a cross-border advisor.
  4. Make the investment and obtain proof of funds and the fund's subscription certificate.
  5. Submit the Golden Visa application via the AIMA Portal ARI with documents and schedule biometrics — budget for the multi-month queue above.
  6. Receive your residence card (initially, then renewed every 2 years), and note the exact issuance date — it's the start of your citizenship clock under the 2026 law.
  7. Maintain ~7 days/year presence, keep the investment in place throughout, and track both the 5-year permanent-residence milestone and the 10-year (or 7-year) citizenship milestone.

Is the Portugal Golden Visa still worth it in 2026?

For Americans who want EU optionality without relocating, it remains one of the strongest programs available — low stay requirement, family inclusion, Schengen access, and a genuine (if now more distant) EU passport at the end. But the value equation has shifted on three fronts at once: the property route is gone, so the entry cost is capital at real investment risk rather than a tangible asset; AIMA's processing queue can add a year or more before your citizenship clock even starts; and Lei Orgânica 1/2026 pushed that clock itself from 5 to 10 years (7 for EU/CPLP nationals) — with a permanent-residence alternative available at year 5 that most marketing pages don't mention. Go in with 2026 numbers, a fund that will actually give you a PFIC statement, clean US tax planning, and a licensed advisor — not a 2022 blog post.

Frequently asked questions

Can Americans still get a Portugal Golden Visa in 2026?

Yes. US citizens are eligible and among the top applicants. The main routes are a €500,000 investment in a CMVM-regulated qualifying fund or a €250,000 (€200,000 in low-density areas) donation supporting arts and cultural heritage. The real-estate option was removed in October 2023.

How much do you need to invest, and what does it cost in government fees?

€500,000 in a qualifying fund, €500,000 in scientific research, or €250,000–€250,000/€200,000 in arts and cultural heritage. On top of the investment, AIMA charges government fees per applicant under Portaria n.º 307/2023 — roughly €632 to file, about €6,314 on approval, and about €3,158 at each 2-year renewal — plus your lawyer's fees and the fund's own subscription costs.

Can you still buy property for the Golden Visa?

No. Since October 2023, real-estate investment no longer qualifies, whether purchased directly or through a fund that holds property. Qualifying routes are funds, research, cultural donations, and job creation.

Is a Golden Visa fund a PFIC for US tax purposes?

Almost always, yes. Most qualifying funds earn passive income (dividends, interest, capital gains, rents), which makes them a Passive Foreign Investment Company under US tax law — triggering annual Form 8621 filing. Ask the fund manager in writing whether they will provide an annual QEF information statement before you subscribe, and get a US cross-border CPA to model the QEF election against the punitive default "excess distribution" regime, which can push your effective rate above 50%.

How long does the process actually take in 2026?

Longer than the statutory targets suggest. Applicants commonly report 6–24 months from filing to a biometrics appointment and a further 6–18 months to card issuance — a realistic total of roughly one to well over three years. AIMA's backlog has triggered an ombudsman complaint on behalf of over a thousand applicants and separate litigation over the 2026 citizenship-law transition.

How long until Portuguese citizenship?

Under Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026 (effective 19 May 2026), the naturalization timeline is 10 years from the date your first residence card was issued for most nationals, or 7 years for EU/EEA and CPLP nationals — plus the existing A2 Portuguese-language requirement and a new civic-knowledge test. Applications filed on or before 18 May 2026 remain under the prior 5-year rule. A separate permanent-residence status remains available after 5 years under the unchanged Aliens Act, short of full citizenship.

Can I bring my family?

Yes. A spouse or de-facto partner, minor children, financially dependent adult children up to about age 26 in full-time education, and dependent parents (generally over 65, or younger if genuinely dependent) can all be included. The investment is made once by the main applicant; AIMA's government fees are charged per family member.

Do I pay Portuguese tax with a Golden Visa?

The visa alone does not make you tax resident; that generally requires 183+ days a year in Portugal or making it your habitual home. US citizens remain subject to US worldwide taxation regardless, and the old NHR pension tax break closed to new entrants on 1 January 2025, replaced by the narrower IFICI regime.

Greece Golden Visa 2026: Cost, Requirements & US Citizen Guide

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated September 2026  ·  12 min read
Greece is one of the few Golden Visas still based on buying real estate. In 2026 the minimum is tiered: €800,000 in Athens, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and larger islands; €400,000 in most other regions; and €250,000 only for restoration or commercial-to-residential conversions. It grants 5-year EU residency with no stay requirement — and you keep a tangible asset.

While Portugal and Spain have closed or gutted their real-estate routes, Greece kept its property-based Golden Visa alive — making it the go-to option for Americans who want an EU residency and a tangible asset rather than a fund subscription. But the rules tightened sharply in 2024–2025, and the entry price now depends heavily on where you buy. Here's the 2026 reality.

What is the Greece Golden Visa?

The Greece Golden Visa is a residency-by-investment program. A qualifying real-estate purchase earns you and your family a renewable 5-year residence permit, the right to live in Greece, and visa-free travel across the Schengen Area. There is no obligation to relocate — the permit has no minimum-stay requirement. You can rent the property out for income while holding it — but only on long lets of 60 days or more: short-term (Airbnb-style) letting of a Golden Visa property is banned under Law 5100/2024.

2026 investment thresholds

This is the part most outdated guides get wrong. The single €250,000 entry point is largely gone; thresholds are now tiered by location and property type:

Minimum investmentWhere it applies
€800,000Athens & Thessaloniki metro areas, Mykonos, Santorini, and islands with population over ~3,100
€400,000All other regions of Greece
€250,000Only properties bought for restoration, or commercial-to-residential conversion

A key structural rule: the investment must be in a single property meeting the threshold — you cannot stack several smaller units to reach it. For the €400,000 and €800,000 tiers, the property must also have a minimum habitable area of 120 square metres (interior floor space, excluding parking and storage). The €250,000 restoration/conversion route is not subject to the 120 sqm minimum. Greece's 2026 procedural statute, Law 5275/2026, left the single-property rule intact — you still cannot pool several smaller units to reach the €400,000 or €800,000 threshold, and each qualifying purchase must be one residential unit of at least 120 m². The same law introduced a 90-day processing guarantee for Golden Visa applications and ended backdating. Additionally, €250K/€400K-tier properties must remain in qualifying use. On top of the purchase price, budget transfer tax, notary and legal fees, and per-family-member application costs.

The rule that quietly kills the "rent it on Airbnb" plan: under Law 5100/2024, a property acquired for the Golden Visa on or after 1 September 2024 cannot be let short-term — anywhere in Greece — for as long as you hold the permit. "Short-term" means letting or subletting for under 60 days, listed on a platform or not. Breaching it can mean a fine of up to €50,000 and revocation of the residence permit. Long lets of 60 days or more are still allowed. Permits granted under earlier frameworks are generally not caught by the ban. Model your Greek yield on long-term rent, not on peak-season nightly rates.

Worked example: what €400,000 and €800,000 actually cost

The headline investment figure is never the whole bill. Budget the Greek transfer tax of 3.09% (levied on the property's objective/assessed value, not necessarily the price you agreed), notary and legal fees of roughly 2–3% combined, and the Golden Visa application fee itself — €2,000 plus a €16 printing fee per main applicant, confirmed on the Ministry of Migration and Asylum's own Golden Visa page, with the same €2,000 + €16 charged again at each 5-year renewal. Family members added to the same application each need their own residence-permit file; get the current per-dependent fee from your lawyer rather than assume it matches the main applicant's, since the published schedule is not itemised by dependent on the ministry's page.

Why Americans choose Greece

  • You keep a real asset: unlike fund-based visas, you own property you can use, long-let, or later sell — but note the short-term-rental ban below: Airbnb-style letting of a Golden Visa property is prohibited.
  • No stay requirement: hold the permit while continuing to live in the US.
  • Whole family included: spouse, dependent children, and dependent parents on one investment.
  • Schengen access and an EU base, with a lower entry price than most Western-European programs (outside the €800K zones).

Golden Visa fees, in full

Beyond the property-purchase costs above, the residence-permit process itself carries its own government fees, confirmed on the Ministry of Migration and Asylum's Golden Visa page:

  • Initial permit (main applicant): €2,000 electronic fee + €16 printing fee = €2,016
  • Renewal (every 5 years): the same €2,000 + €16, for as long as you keep the qualifying investment
  • Family members: each dependent added to the application needs their own residence-permit file and fee; confirm the current per-dependent amount with your lawyer, since the ministry's published fee schedule does not break this out

On top of the government fee, budget for a Greek lawyer to handle the application (typically a fixed engagement fee separate from the property-purchase legal work), certified translations of your documents, an apostille on your US-issued paperwork, and health insurance valid in Greece for every applicant — a mandatory supporting document, not an optional extra.

Residency, stay rules & the citizenship reality

The permit is valid for 5 years and renews as long as you keep the qualifying investment. Crucially, there is no minimum-stay requirement to maintain residency — ideal for a "plan B."

Citizenship is a different and much longer story. The Golden Visa does not grant Greek citizenship. To naturalize you must become a genuine resident — living in Greece at least 183 days per year for 7 years — and meet language and integration requirements. For most American investors, Greece is a residency and Schengen-access play, not a fast passport.

What the permit does not give you: the right to work as an employee in Greece. Consistent with the investor-permit category under Greece's Immigration and Social Integration Code (the "art. 20B" investor permit referenced on the Ministry's own Golden Visa page), the Golden Visa is a residence permit, not a work permit — it does not authorise dependent employment (being hired by a Greek employer) for the main investor. Running your own business, self-employment, and passive income (rent, dividends, capital gains) are a different matter and are not blocked by the permit itself, but they carry their own Greek tax and, in some cases, separate licensing requirements. If working in Greece as someone else's employee is part of your plan, that needs a different residence-permit category entirely — raise it with your immigration lawyer before you invest, not after.

The realistic citizenship math. Seven years of genuine residence — 183+ days a year, actually living in Greece, not just renewing paperwork from the US — plus a Greek-language and civic-integration test is a materially different commitment from "buy a condo, get a passport." For the large majority of MigrateState readers who plan to keep their US home base and visit Greece for weeks or months a year, citizenship is not a realistic outcome of this program, and the 5-year renewable residence permit with no minimum-stay requirement is the actual product on offer — a genuinely valuable EU/Schengen foothold in its own right, just not a passport shortcut.

US & Greek tax impact

Holding the Golden Visa does not make you a Greek tax resident. You become tax resident only if you spend more than 183 days a year in Greece or make it your main home — which the program never requires. Most Golden Visa holders who keep their US life intact stay non-resident for Greek tax purposes and are taxed by Greece only on Greek-source income — principally the rent.

ENFIA: the annual property tax every owner pays

Owning Greek real estate — Golden Visa or not — means an annual ENFIA (Uniform Real Estate Property Tax) bill, administered by AADE, Greece's independent tax authority. ENFIA combines a main tax calculated from the property's official "objective value" (location, size, age, floor) with a supplementary tax that applies above a total-property-value threshold. It is assessed and paid every year for as long as you own the property, via an annual E9 property declaration on file with AADE. The exact current rate table and supplementary-tax threshold could not be confirmed against an AADE-published rate schedule in this session — budget a modest but real annual line item and get the specific figure for your property from a Greek accountant or your lawyer; see the flag below.

Rental income tax and the two special regimes worth knowing about

Rental income earned by a non-resident owner is taxed by Greece on a progressive scale that tops out at a marginal rate of 45% on the highest bracket of rental income, with a standard flat-rate deduction for maintenance costs applied before tax. The exact bracket breakpoints and the middle-tier rate could not be confirmed against a current, unambiguous official source in this session (secondary sources vary on the exact cut-offs) — get the current scale from a Greek accountant before you model net yield; see the flag below.

Two alternative regimes exist for people who actually relocate their tax residence to Greece — not the typical Golden Visa buyer who stays US-resident, but relevant if you are weighing an eventual move: Article 5A of the Greek Income Tax Code lets a new Greek tax resident pay a flat €100,000 per year on all foreign-source income, for up to 15 tax years, provided they make a qualifying investment in Greece of at least €500,000 within three years of applying — confirmed directly on an official AADE guidance document. Article 5B offers foreign pensioners a flat 7% rate on foreign-source pension income for up to 15 tax years, provided the applicant was not a Greek tax resident for 5 of the prior 6 years and is transferring tax residence from a country with an administrative-cooperation agreement with Greece — also confirmed on the same official AADE document. Neither regime is what a typical Golden Visa investor needs (they are for people becoming Greek tax residents, which the Golden Visa does not require), but they are worth knowing if the property is a first step toward an eventual full relocation.

The US side: full worldwide taxation, with no relief from Greece's zero-residency design

As a US citizen you remain taxed on worldwide income regardless of the Golden Visa. The practical mechanics: rental income from the Greek property is reportable on your US return (Schedule E), converted to dollars under the normal rules; a future sale is a US-reportable capital gain (Form 8949/Schedule D); and a Greek bank account — typically needed to receive rent or pay ENFIA — can trigger FBAR (FinCEN Form 114) once your aggregate foreign accounts exceed $10,000, with Form 8938 (FATCA) potentially applying above higher thresholds. Because Greece also taxes the same rental income, a Foreign Tax Credit claimed on Form 1116 is generally available to offset US tax on the same income and avoid double taxation — model both countries' computations together, since the credit is capped at the US tax attributable to that foreign-source income, not a dollar-for-dollar refund of what Greece collected. One structural advantage versus fund-based golden visas: direct real estate ownership avoids the messy US PFIC (Passive Foreign Investment Company) rules that can apply to foreign investment funds — a real nuance worth weighing if you're choosing between Greece's direct-property route and a Portugal-style fund route.

Step-by-step: applying as an American

  1. Get a Greek tax number (AFM) and open a Greek bank account.
  2. Identify the right zone & threshold for your target property (€250K/€400K/€800K).
  3. Engage an independent Greek lawyer and run title/zoning due diligence.
  4. Purchase the property via a notarial deed and register it.
  5. Submit the Golden Visa application with proof of funds, criminal-record certificate, and health insurance.
  6. Provide biometrics and receive the 5-year residence permits for you and your family.
  7. Plan your US tax compliance (rental reporting, FBAR) before year-end.

Is the Greece Golden Visa worth it in 2026?

For Americans who want EU optionality plus a usable Mediterranean asset, Greece remains one of the strongest options — especially in the €400K regions away from the premium zones. Just go in with current thresholds, a single qualifying property, and clean US tax planning. The "€250K beach apartment in Santorini" era is over; the smart 2026 play is a well-chosen property in a €400K region or a genuine restoration project.

The €400K-versus-€800K decision usually comes down to one question: do you actually want the Athens/Thessaloniki/Mykonos/Santorini lifestyle and rental market, or would a genuinely good property in a €400K region (Crete outside its main resort towns, the Peloponnese, most of northern Greece, many islands under the population cutoff) serve the same "EU foothold plus vacation home" goal for half the capital outlay? Run both the worked-example cost table above and the ongoing annual carrying cost — ENFIA, any building/community fees, insurance, and the trustee-free maintenance a directly-owned property still needs — before deciding, since the premium-zone properties also tend to carry materially higher annual ENFIA and management costs, not just a higher entry price. Whichever tier you choose, the discipline is the same: confirm the zone and the 120 m² test against the specific parcel before you sign, register the sources you relied on, and treat the Golden Visa application fee and lawyer's engagement as a separate, budgeted line item from the property purchase itself — not an afterthought that surprises you at closing.

Frequently asked questions

How much do you need for the Greece Golden Visa in 2026?

Tiered: €800,000 in Athens, Thessaloniki, Mykonos, Santorini and larger islands; €400,000 in most other regions; €250,000 only for restoration or commercial-to-residential conversions.

Can Americans get the Greece Golden Visa?

Yes. US citizens are non-EU nationals and fully eligible. You qualify via a property investment meeting the zone minimum, plus proof of funds, a criminal-record certificate, and health insurance.

Is there a stay requirement?

No. The 5-year permit has no minimum-stay requirement. Qualifying for citizenship later requires 183+ days per year for 7 years.

Does it lead to citizenship?

Not directly. Citizenship requires becoming a genuine resident (183+ days/year for 7 years) and meeting naturalization rules including language.

Can I rent out a Greece Golden Visa property?

Long-term, yes; short-term, no. Under Law 5100/2024, a property acquired for the Golden Visa from 1 September 2024 cannot be let on a short-term basis (under 60 days, platform-listed or not) anywhere in Greece for as long as you hold the permit. Penalties reach €50,000 and can include revocation of the permit. Leases of 60 days or more remain permitted, so build your yield model on long lets.

Can I include my family?

Yes. One qualifying investment can include your spouse, dependent children, and dependent parents.

What does the Golden Visa itself cost in government fees?

€2,000 plus a €16 printing fee for the main applicant's initial permit, confirmed on the Ministry of Migration and Asylum's Golden Visa page, and the same amount again at each 5-year renewal. Each family member needs their own residence-permit file; confirm the current per-dependent fee with your lawyer.

Does the Golden Visa let me work in Greece?

Not as an employee. It is a residence permit, not a work permit, and does not authorise dependent employment for the main investor. Running your own business or earning passive income is a different matter, with its own Greek tax and licensing rules.

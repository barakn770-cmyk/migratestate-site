Buying Property in San Miguel de Allende as an American (2026): Colonial Homes, INAH Rules, Costs and Tax

MS By The MigrateState Editorial Team · Reviewed by a licensed international real-estate attorney  ·  Updated November 2026  ·  14 min read
San Miguel de Allende is not a beach, and the holding structure is not the one used in Los Cabos. Outside Mexico's restricted zone an American can take direct title after a convenio filed with the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores. The SRE's 2026 schedule lists MXN 5,250 for that filing, not the MXN 21,650 fideicomiso permit. The Municipality of San Miguel de Allende charges a progressive acquisition tax under its 2026 income law, up to 4% on the slice above MXN 1,500,000.01. The state municipal finance law, in force from the day after 8 July 2026, reduces the value by ten annual UMAs on every operation and gives an appraisal one year, not 90 days. Inside a 0.75 km² historic-monument zone, renovation needs INAH. The deed is not a visa, and it does not turn off US tax.

San Miguel is sold to Americans as a colonial town with a large retiree community and houses listed in dollars. The statute that actually hits the deed is narrower than that market. Title, for a parcel outside the restricted zone, is direct. The transfer tax is a municipal tariff for this one municipality, not a Guanajuato statewide percentage you can copy from a neighboring town. And the renovation rules that make a centro house difficult live in a 1982 federal decree and in INAH's permit, not in the acquisition-tax schedule. The national map is in the Mexico buying guide. The coastal contrast is the Los Cabos guide and the fideicomiso guide. This page is the San Miguel filing, the 2026 tariff, and the US return that still applies when the bank trust is not in the file.

Direct title, not a coastal fideicomiso

Article 27 of the Constitution bars a foreigner from holding direct title to land within 50 kilometers of the coast or 100 kilometers of a border. San Miguel de Allende is an interior municipality of Guanajuato. It is not on the sea and it is not on the border. The path the Constitution describes for property outside that strip is direct ownership, coupled with an agreement, made before the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores, to be considered a Mexican national with respect to the property and not to invoke the protection of a foreign government. That agreement is the Calvo clause. It is not a fideicomiso.

The SRE cost page, marked as information in force from 1 January 2026 and sourced by the SRE to Anexo 19 of the 2026 miscellaneous tax resolution, lists MXN 5,250 for the reception, study, and where granted the approval of the convenio de renuncia for a foreign natural person acquiring property outside the restricted zone. The same amount is listed for a foreign legal entity. The page lists a different fee, MXN 21,650, to issue a permit to constitute a fideicomiso inside the restricted zone. Using the Los Cabos number on a San Miguel purchase is the wrong filing. The SRE convenio page says that from 17 June 2026 it accepts only forms SRE-2022-084-006-A and SRE-2022-084-006-B, as published in the Diario Oficial de la Federación on 16 June 2026. The file has to show the property's area, measures, and boundaries, the form of acquisition, and a migratory document that proves a stay condition allowing the purchase. This guide does not say which migratory document qualifies. The SRE page also says that if there is doubt whether the parcel is inside the restricted zone, the SRE consults INEGI, and the applicant should add geographic coordinates. Ask the notario to put that measurement in the file. Do not let a listing decide it.

The 2026 acquisition-tax tariff

The rate is not a state flat percentage. Article 7 of the Ley de Ingresos para el Municipio de San Miguel de Allende, Guanajuato, for fiscal year 2026 (H. Congreso del Estado, published in the Periódico Oficial number 260, 21st part, 30 December 2025) sets this tariff. The income law says the tax is computed by subtracting the bracket's lower limit from the base, applying the rate to that excess, and adding the fixed quota.

The 4% is not a flat rate on the whole price. It applies only to the excess over MXN 1,500,000.01, on top of the fixed quota of MXN 39,125. A purchase that never leaves the first bracket is 2% with a zero quota. Article 7 does not add a foreigner surcharge. Article 50 of the same income law sets a separate rate of 0.5% when the acquisition is a gift or an inheritance between spouses or relatives in the direct line, ascending or descending. That is a rate, not an exemption.

The ten-UMA reduction, and a worked example

The tariff is applied to a base the income law does not invent by itself. Decreto Número 202, the Ley Hacendaria Municipal para el Estado de Guanajuato, was published in the Periódico Oficial number 135, third part, on 8 July 2026, and entered into force the next day. It abrogates the Ley de Hacienda para los Municipios del Estado de Guanajuato of 26 December 1989. Matters already in process on that day finish under the old law. From the entry into force, references in other laws to the old hacienda law are read as references to this one, which is how the 2026 income law's cross-reference is read now.

Article 42 says the base is the highest of the registered fiscal value, the operation value, and an appraisal by a perito authorized by the municipal treasury or by a certified valuation unit. A bank appraisal is also allowed. Article 338, in the chapter on this tax, says that for every operation the value is reduced by ten times the annual value of the UMA. Fractions II and III raise that reduction to fifteen times, for defined social-interest housing and for houses bought with listed public housing credit, and to twenty times the daily UMA raised to a year, for popular housing under the cap in the Código Territorial. Those larger reductions apply only if the home is the buyer's only property or possession. Otherwise the reduction falls back to ten. Article 339 gives the reduction once per unit or operation. Buying an adjoining parcel, or part of one you already hold, within two years does not get it again. In a building of dwellings the reduction is per dwelling. The article says that sentence does not apply to hotels.

INEGI's 8 January 2026 bulletin sets the UMA in force from 1 February 2026 at MXN 117.31 a day, MXN 3,566.22 a month, and MXN 42,794.64 a year. Ten times the annual figure is MXN 427,946.40. That amount comes off the value. It is not a credit against the tax of MXN 427,946.40.

Appraisal, 30 days, and the notario's declaration

Article 42 gives the appraisal one year from the date of its authorization. A buyer who just read the Los Cabos page should not reuse that municipality's 90-day window, and a buyer who just read the Tulum page should not reuse Quintana Roo's 180-day window. Each state wrote its own period. Article 44 requires payment within 30 days counted from the date the contract or the instrument of transfer is granted, on the official form, at the Tesorería Municipal. Article 45 says that if the act is in a public deed, the notario signs and files the declaration. A deed granted outside the state is filed by any interested party with a certified copy. A transfer that comes from a court judgment is filed by the taxpayer with a certified copy of the judgment.

Article 40 makes the taxpayer any individual or entity that acquires property in the state, or a real right attached to it, by any title. Article 41's list of what counts as an acquisition includes a sale, a gift, an inheritance, a contribution to a company, a merger, a trust in the cases the article spells out, and a court award. The ordinary San Miguel purchase this page is about is the direct transfer, not a fideicomiso. The notario fee and the annual predial bill are not in article 7. This page does not invent either one as a percentage of the price.

The tariff is the easy table. The zone and the monument perimeter are the file.

A notario who is not the seller's notario should confirm the restricted-zone measurement, which of the three values is highest, and whether the house sits inside the 1982 perimeter before you price a renovation.

Annual predial is a different tax

The acquisition tax is paid because the property changed hands. Predial is paid because you hold it. Article 4 of the same 2026 income law sets the urban predial, for property inside the built-up area of a population center that has buildings, on this marginal scale. The bimonthly bill is the excess over the lower limit, times the marginal rate, plus the fixed quota, divided by six. The annual bill is that bimonthly result times six.

Suburban property with buildings, outside the built-up area but inside the growth area, is a flat 0.207% under the next fraction of article 4, not this table. Article 49 sets the minimum annual predial for 2026 at MXN 349.48. A taxpayer who pays the whole year in January 2026 gets 15% off, and one who pays in February gets 10% off, except anyone already at the minimum quota. The Tesorería applies article 335's peso rounding. This page does not publish a single "typical" annual bill, because the fiscal value is not the contract price and the bracket is not a flat 0.354% on the whole value.

Water is a separate account. The articles of the income law and the Ley Hacendaria Municipal read for this guide set predial and the acquisition tax. They do not set a municipal water tariff, a connection fee, or a scarcity statistic. Ask the municipal water utility for the account on the specific house. A sentence in a listing about the town's water is not that account.

INAH and the 0.75 km² monument zone

The colonial center is not a marketing label only. A decree of 28 July 1982 declared a zona de monumentos históricos in the city of San Miguel de Allende. Article 2 of that decree describes an area of 0.75 square kilometers, with a perimeter drawn along the axes of named streets. The decree was issued under the Ley Federal sobre Monumentos y Zonas Arqueológicos, Artísticos e Históricos, and it cites that law's article 42 among its bases. INAH's permit page says that, under the same federal law and its regulation, work on a building that is a historic monument, and work in the situations the related permit pages cover — a building next to a monument, and a building inside a historic-monument zone that is neither — requires the institute's authorization. The permit is a file: plans, a description of the work, proof of ownership, photographs. This guide does not quote a permit fee, because the decree does not set one.

The practical line is the perimeter. A house inside those street axes is in the zone even when the facade has already been altered. A house in a later colonia, outside the 0.75 square kilometers, is not in that decree. The municipality's own building permit is a different authorization from INAH's. Getting one does not supply the other. Read the deed's location against the decree's perimeter before you budget a kitchen, a roof, a new opening, or a paint color as if it were an ordinary remodel. The decree does not set a construction price, and this page will not invent a price per square meter for the centro or for anywhere else in the municipality.

US tax when there is no fideicomiso

IRS Revenue Ruling 2013-14 holds that a standard Mexican fideicomiso used to hold a personal residence is not a trust for United States tax purposes. That ruling matters in Los Cabos, where the coast forces the trust. It is not the holding structure in an ordinary San Miguel purchase. Direct title means there is no Mexican bank trust to classify. It does not mean the house drops off the US return. Rent is still US income. How a foreign residential rental is depreciated is in the 30-year ADS guide. Mexico-specific reporting is in US taxes on Mexican property. A sale has its own Mexican computation, covered in selling property in Mexico as a US citizen.

The house is not an FBAR asset. A Mexican bank account used to buy it, collect rent, or pay predial can be. The FBAR line is $10,000 aggregate, at any point in the year. Form 8938 starts higher and depends on where you live and how you file. Both are explained in the FBAR guide and the Form 8938 guide. Buying the house does not make you a Mexican tax resident and does not make you a Mexican immigration resident. The income and savings tests for a temporary resident visa are in the temporary resident visa guide. The deed and the SRE convenio are not that application.

Step-by-step

  1. Hire your own notario, not the seller's, before you sign.
  2. Confirm the parcel is outside the restricted zone. If there is any doubt, the SRE file needs coordinates so INEGI can be asked. Outside the strip, the filing is the convenio at MXN 5,250, not a fideicomiso at MXN 21,650.
  3. Plot the house against the 1982 perimeter before you treat a renovation as ordinary work. Inside 0.75 square kilometers, INAH is in the file.
  4. Take the highest of fiscal value, contract, and appraisal. Article 42 gives the appraisal one year from authorization.
  5. Have the notario apply article 338 — ten annual UMAs, MXN 427,946.40 at the 2026 annual UMA — and then article 7's tariff. On the MXN 10,000,000 illustration that is MXN 362,007 after article 335.
  6. Pay within 30 days of the instrument. The notario files the declaration when the act is a public deed.
  7. If you will live there, start residency separately. The escritura is not a visa, and it does not change the US return.

If you're not American

Article 27, the SRE convenio, the San Miguel tariff, the ten-UMA reduction, the one-year appraisal, and the INAH zone apply to a foreign buyer regardless of passport. The FBAR, Form 8938, and the way a directly owned foreign house sits on a US return are United States rules. If you are not a US person, ask an adviser in your own country what a Mexican bank account and a Mexican sale have to be reported as at home.

Frequently asked questions

Can an American take title in their own name in San Miguel de Allende?

San Miguel de Allende is in the interior of Guanajuato, so the usual path is direct title, not a coastal fideicomiso. Article 27 of the Constitution lets a foreigner acquire property outside the restricted zone — 50 kilometers from the coast and 100 kilometers from a border — if they agree before the Secretaría de Relaciones Exteriores to be treated as a Mexican national regarding that property and not to invoke their government's protection. The SRE's 2026 fee schedule lists MXN 5,250 for that convenio for a natural person. If the parcel's location is in doubt, the SRE consults INEGI using coordinates. The notario measures. A sales office does not.

What acquisition tax does San Miguel charge in 2026?

Article 7 of the Ley de Ingresos for the Municipality of San Miguel de Allende for 2026 sets a progressive tariff. The rate is 2% up to MXN 500,000, then 2.25%, 2.50%, 2.75%, 3% and 3.50% through successive brackets, and 4% on the excess over MXN 1,500,000.01, with a fixed quota in each bracket. The tariff does not add a foreigner surcharge. Gifts and inheritances between spouses or direct-line relatives use 0.5% under article 50 of the same law.

How does the ten-UMA reduction change the bill?

Article 338 of the Ley Hacendaria Municipal para el Estado de Guanajuato reduces the value on every acquisition by ten times the annual UMA. INEGI's annual UMA from 1 February 2026 is MXN 42,794.64, so the reduction is MXN 427,946.40. On an illustrative value of MXN 10,000,000 the reduced base is MXN 9,572,053.60. Applying the top bracket and rounding under article 335 produces MXN 362,007 of tax. Larger reductions, of fifteen or twenty annual UMAs, apply only to defined social-interest and popular housing, and only if it is the buyer's only property.

Is the appraisal window 90 days, as in Los Cabos?

No. Article 42 of the Guanajuato municipal finance law says the base is the highest of the registered fiscal value, the operation value, and an appraisal, and that the appraisal is valid for one year from the date of its authorization. Los Cabos wrote 90 days into its own municipal law. Do not reuse that window here. The tax is due within 30 days of the contract, and the notario files the declaration when the act is in a public deed.

When does INAH have to approve work on the house?

A decree of 28 July 1982 declared a historic-monument zone in the city of San Miguel de Allende covering 0.75 square kilometers, drawn along street axes in the historic center. The decree was issued under the federal monuments law. INAH's current permit pages say work on a historic monument, on a building next to one, or on a building inside a historic-monument zone needs the institute's authorization. A house outside that perimeter is a different file. The decree does not cover the whole municipality, and it does not set a renovation fee this guide can quote.

Does the deed give me Mexican residency?

No. The SRE convenio and the escritura are not a visa. Temporary residency is a separate application. Buying the house does not change how the United States taxes you.

Does Revenue Ruling 2013-14 apply if there is no fideicomiso?

That ruling is about a standard Mexican bank trust holding a personal residence. Direct title in San Miguel is not that trust, so the ruling is not the holding structure. The house is still foreign real estate on a US return. Rent and a later sale are reported. The house itself is not an FBAR asset. A Mexican bank account used to buy it or pay the bills can be, once foreign accounts together exceed $10,000 at any point in the year.

The expensive version of an overseas property scam is rarely a fake listing with stock photos. It is a real building, a real beach, and a payment path that does not match the document that will be registered. The buyer wires because someone they have been emailing for weeks tells them the account changed, or because a "certificate of possession" is presented as if it were title, or because stage payments for an unfinished home go to the developer with no guarantee and no segregated account. The checks below are the ones an official text actually describes. Where a number is not in those texts, it is not on this page.

What the FBI numbers count

The FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center publishes one annual report of complaints the public files at ic3.gov. The 2025 report's crime-type table, and its three-year comparison, print these totals. Appendix C of the same report says each complaint has only one crime type, and that descriptive data can change as a complaint is reviewed. These are reported complaints and reported losses. They are not a count of purchases, and they are not labeled as foreign property.

The definition in the report's appendix is short: real-estate fraud is "loss of funds from a real estate investment or fraud involving rental or timeshare property." Business email compromise is the broader pattern in which a legitimate transfer is redirected, including by a compromised or spoofed email. A closing wire can be filed under either heading. Adding the two 2025 losses would double-count nothing only if no reader treated them as one pot. They are different rows. Use them that way.

The wire instruction

The mechanism the 2025 report actually narrates is a changed instruction at the moment of closing. In March 2025 the Recovery Asset Team received a complaint from a Missouri buyer who was closing and got a compromised email from the "title company" with wire instructions for over $1.3 million. Part of the money was then directed onward: the report says the recipient-account holder was told to send $1 million to a fraudulent account in Hong Kong, and the team initiated an international freeze through FinCEN and the FBI's legal attaché. In August 2025 the same team describes a business-email incident it also labels real estate: buyers closing on a home received an email impersonating their attorneys, a wire for over $449,000 reached the recipient bank, and after the complaint the recipient bank reported that the full amount was still in the account and on hold.

Both stories are domestic closings in the report. They are on this page because the instruction channel is the same one an overseas buyer is already using: email, a person they have not met this week, and an account number that is tedious to check. The State Department's scams page states the general rule in one line: never send money to someone abroad if you have not met them in person, especially if the contact started online. A developer you have met is not that sentence. A new account, in a new email, from a display name you already trust, is the pattern in the IC3 cases. Call the notary, the bank or the lawyer on a number you already had, and read the account back. Do not reply to the email that brought the new instructions.

Our guide to moving the purchase money covers the banking side of a legitimate transfer. It is not a substitute for matching the destination account to the contract and the registry file.

Mexico: ejido land is not a deed

The Ley Agraria, published in the Diario Oficial on 26 February 1992 and current through the reform published on 14 November 2025, is the statute that regulates Article 27 of the Constitution on agrarian property. Article 9 says the ejido, as a population nucleus, has its own legal personality and owns the lands it was granted. That is the starting point a "certificate of possession" skips. The person offering the parcel is often an ejidatario, a holder of ejido rights, not the owner of a private lot that can be deeded to a foreigner.

Who can receive those rights is narrow. Article 80 says ejidatarios may transfer their parcel rights to other ejidatarios or avecindados of the same nucleus. The transfer needs a written statement before two witnesses, ratified before a notary (fedatario público), notice to the spouse or partner and the children, who have a 30-day right of first refusal, and written notice to the ejido commission. The Registro Agrario Nacional then inscribes it and issues new parcel certificates. Article 13 defines an avecindado as a Mexican adult who has lived for a year or more on the lands of the nucleus and has been recognized by the assembly or the competent agrarian court. Article 15 says that becoming an ejidatario requires Mexican nationality. A foreign buyer is outside that class. A contract that calls itself a sale of the parcel to a foreigner, while the land is still on the agrarian register, is not the transaction article 80 describes.

Use by a third party is a different contract, and it is not ownership. Article 45 allows the nucleus, or the parcel holder, to make association or use contracts. Contracts that let third parties use ejido land last for the project and not more than 30 years, and they can be extended. A 30-year use right is not dominio pleno, and it is not what a bank will record as the buyer's property.

The path out is dominio pleno, and it is two decisions, not a stamp. Article 23, fraction IX, puts the assembly's authorization of dominio pleno on the list of matters only the assembly can decide. Article 81 says that authorization is available once most parcels have been delimited and assigned under article 56. For the matters in fractions VII to XIV of article 23, which include this one, article 26 requires three-quarters of the ejidatarios on a first call and half plus one on a second call. Article 27 requires a two-thirds vote of those present. Article 28 requires a representative of the Procuraduría Agraria and a notary, and it says assemblies held in breach of that article are void. Article 30 says that for these matters an ejidatario cannot send a proxy. Article 31 says the minutes go before the notary, are signed by the Procuraduría representative, and are inscribed in the Registro Agrario Nacional.

Article 82 is the sentence that decides whether anyone outside the ejido bought land. After the assembly's resolution, the interested ejidatario asks the Registro Agrario Nacional to take the parcel off that register. The RAN issues the property title, and that title is inscribed in the local Registro Público de la Propiedad. From the cancellation in the RAN, the land stops being ejidal and falls under ordinary civil law. Until that cancellation, it has not. Article 84 then gives family, long-time workers of the parcel, other ejidatarios, avecindados and the nucleus, in that order, a 30-day right of first refusal on the first sale to an outsider. If the notice is not given, the sale can be annulled. Article 86 says that first sale to someone outside the nucleus is free of federal tax for the seller and must be at least at the reference price set by the Comisión de Avalúos de Bienes Nacionales or a credit institution. This page does not convert that reference price into dollars. The statute does not print one.

The RAN's public padrón of agrarian nuclei is PHINA. It is a place to see the nucleus. It is not, by itself, the article 82 cancellation and the Registro Público inscription. Ask the notario to pull both. A fideicomiso does not repair a missing cancellation: the bank trust in our fideicomiso guide is how a foreigner holds rights inside the restricted zone after there is private title to put into the trust. Article 27 of the Constitution, in the text published by the Instituto Nacional de Lenguas Indígenas, still says that in a strip of 100 kilometers along the borders and 50 kilometers along the beaches, foreigners may not acquire direct ownership of lands and waters at all. Tulum is the place on this site where that sequence is the whole story; the city guide is buying in Tulum, and the national frame is buying in Mexico.

Spain: the off-plan guarantee and the nota simple

Spain does have a statute aimed at the stage-payment problem, and it is limited to housing. Additional provision 1 of Ley 38/1999, of 5 November, on building regulation, as modified by final provision 3.2 of Ley 20/2015, applies to individuals and companies promoting the construction of any kind of housing, including housing in a community of owners or a cooperative, who intend to take money from buyers for that construction. The BOE record for Ley 20/2015 states that the law entered into force on 1 January 2016. The consolidated text of Ley 38/1999 is the wording used here.

From the moment the building licence is obtained, the promoter must guarantee return of the amounts delivered plus legal interest, if construction does not start or does not come to a successful end within the agreed delivery period. The guarantee is either a surety-insurance contract with an insurer authorized to operate in Spain, or a solidary bank guarantee from an authorized credit institution. The advances have to be received through a credit institution and deposited in a special account, separated from any other funds of the promoter, and the promoter may draw on that account only for the building of the homes. The credit institution, on its own responsibility, must require that guarantee before it opens the account. The guarantee extends to the amounts the buyers paid, including applicable taxes, plus legal interest.

The insurance has to be an individual policy for each buyer, identifying the home. The promoter is the policyholder and pays the premium until the purchase is raised to a public deed. The buyer is the insured. The insurer cannot use against the buyer the defenses it would have against the promoter, and non-payment of the premium is never a defense. The policy lasts at least as long as the commitment to build and deliver. If the build does not start or does not finish on time, the buyer formally demands return of the amounts, taxes and interest. If the promoter has not returned them within 30 days, the buyer claims from the insurer, which must indemnify within 30 days of that claim. The buyer can go straight to the insurer when a prior claim on the promoter is not possible. Amounts that were insured but never actually paid by the buyer are not indemnified.

The bank-guarantee path has the same 30-day demand on the promoter, and then a claim on the bank. It also has a deadline buyers miss: if two years pass from the promoter's breach without the buyer having demanded rescission and return of the advances, the aval lapses. That sentence is in the statute. It is not a grace period to negotiate.

The contract for a home that takes advances has to state the obligation to return the money plus legal interest if the build does not start or finish on the agreed dates, or if the habitation certificate or first-occupancy licence is not obtained. It has to identify the insurance or the guarantee and name the insurer or the guarantor. It has to name the credit institution and the account the buyer will pay into. When the purchase contract is granted, the promoter delivers a document proving the guarantee, individualized to the amounts to be advanced. Advertising of a promotion that takes money before or during construction must say that the promoter will comply, and must name the guarantor and the institution where the special account is open. Failing to constitute the guarantee is a consumer infringement and can draw a sanction of up to 25 percent of the amounts whose return should have been secured, or the sanction set by the autonomous community's own rules. Both alternatives are in the sentence. It is not a flat 25 percent in every region.

None of that tells you the seller owns the land. The Registro de la Propiedad does. The Colegio de Registradores describes the nota simple as identifying the property, the holders of the rights inscribed on it (full ownership, mortgage, usufruct and others), and the extent, nature and limits of those rights. Order it from the registry's own site or at the registry, and state a legitimate interest if you are not the holder. A nota simple the seller forwards is a copy of a copy. The coastal application of the guarantee, including Andalucía, is in the Costa del Sol guide; the national purchase steps are in buying property in Spain.

Portugal: the certidão permanente

This page does not state a Portuguese equivalent of the Spanish bank-guarantee rule. No such statute was the text used here, and inventing one would be worse than the gap. What the Portuguese state does publish, and what a buyer can pull before paying, is the land-registry certificate.

The government service "Consultar uma certidão permanente do registo predial," updated on the page as of 19 October 2026 and run by the Instituto dos Registos e do Notariado, says the permanent certificate contains the registrations in force on a property and the registration applications that are pending. Anyone with the access code can consult it while it is valid. The same page says the certificate is valid for six months and is always updated, and it points to Decreto-Lei n.º 116/2008, of 4 July, and Portaria n.º 1513/2008, of 23 December. Pending applications matter: a sale, a mortgage or an attachment that has been filed and not yet completed still shows. A seller who offers last year's paper certificate is offering a document this service was built to replace.

The national purchase mechanics, including the tax side, stay in buying property in Portugal. The certificate tells you the state of the register. It does not price the property, and this page does not quote a fee for it. The Azores service desk publishes a price; the national page used here does not, so no national price is stated.

Power of attorney abuse

A power of attorney is how most overseas closings actually happen, and the form each country will accept is covered in the power-of-attorney guide. The abuse is not the existence of the document. It is the scope, and who wrote it. A special power that names one property by its registry reference, lists the acts (sign this deed, pay this tax, register this title), caps the price, and expires, is a tool. A general power, open-ended, prepared by the developer or the selling agent, that lets the holder sell, mortgage, or direct where the purchase money goes, is a second set of keys to the money and the land.

Two limits follow from the statutes already on this page, without adding a new one. In Mexico, article 30 of the Ley Agraria says an ejidatario cannot send a proxy to the assembly that decides dominio pleno. A power of attorney signed in Houston does not cast that vote. In Spain, the person who should be holding the guarantee document is the buyer named on the individual policy, not an agent whose only copy of the policy arrived as a scan. Read the power for three lines before anyone uses it: the property identifier, the maximum price, and whether the holder can be paid.

Fake escrow, and one FTC case

"Escrow" is a word sellers use for any account that is not their operating account this week. The Spanish statute is specific about what the word has to mean for housing advances: a special account at a credit institution, separated from the promoter's other funds, opened only after the bank has required the guarantee, and named in the contract and in the advertising. An account in the developer's name, or in the name of a related company, is not that account. For Mexico, this page does not cite a statute that forces pre-construction deposits into a third-party account. If the contract does not create one, the buyer is an unsecured creditor of the developer. Say that out loud before treating a "reservation" as a purchase.

The Federal Trade Commission's October 2023 report to Congress on the SAFE WEB Act describes one overseas development sold to Americans in those terms. The Commission shut down a real-estate investment scam known as Sanctuary Belize that took in more than $100 million, which the report calls the largest such scheme the FTC has targeted. The report says SAFE WEB's extraterritorial authority was what let the FTC stop it, and that in August 2023 the FTC began the first phase of redress by sending refunds of approximately $10 million to 1,198 defrauded investors. The report is about the agency's authority and the redress, not a how-to. The fact for a buyer is smaller: a development marketed from the United States, with land in another country, can be a consumer-fraud case, and the completion story is not title.

The State Department's country pages say the same thing without a dollar figure. The Honduras page tells US citizens to exercise extreme caution before investing in real estate, and says fraudulent deeds are common and have led to numerous disputes, with threats and violence used against US citizens in property disputes. The Panama page says foreign buyers have reported broken contracts, unexpected fees, threats of violence, and other claims of fraud and corruption, and that the embassy has received numerous property-dispute complaints, including lost property, broken contracts, demands for additional payments, accusations of fraud and corruption, and occasionally threats of violence. Neither page publishes a rate or a loss total. Neither is quoted here as one.

If the money has already left

The State Department's scams page tells a victim to file with the Federal Trade Commission and with the FBI at ic3.gov. The 2025 IC3 report describes why the same day matters for a wire. The Recovery Asset Team, which took on domestic-to-international transactions in April 2024, uses the Financial Fraud Kill Chain with banks to freeze funds. In the August 2025 closing case, the complaint produced a freeze while the full amount of a wire over $449,000 was still at the recipient bank. In the March 2025 case, part of a wire over $1.3 million had already been directed toward Hong Kong, and the team went to FinCEN and the legal attaché. The report presents these as cases, not as a recovery rate for property buyers. File anyway, and call the sending bank the same hour. A recall after the money has left the second bank is a different problem from a freeze while it is still there.

If you're not American

The agrarian statute, the Spanish guarantee and the Portuguese certificate do not ask for a US passport. Article 80's limit is about who may hold ejido rights, not about which foreign country the outsider comes from. The nota simple and the certidão permanente are public registry products. The IC3 report says the center received complaints from more than 200 countries in 2025. The US reporting that sits around a purchase — the account the wire lands in, the tax return, the estate — is a US-person question and is handled in the tax guides on this site, not in the registry extract.

Frequently asked questions

Do the FBI's real-estate loss figures measure fraud on foreign property?

No. The 2025 IC3 report defines real-estate fraud as loss of funds from a real-estate investment or fraud involving rental or timeshare property. It does not say those complaints were purchases abroad. In 2025 that category was 12,368 complaints and $275,110,419 in reported losses. Business email compromise is a separate crime type, 24,768 complaints and $3,046,598,558, and each complaint has only one crime type, so the two totals are not added. The report's own closing examples include US closings in which a spoofed email changed the wire instructions.

Can a foreigner buy ejido land in Mexico?

Not by taking the parcel rights. Article 80 of the Ley Agraria lets an ejidatario transfer parcel rights only to other ejidatarios or avecindados of the same nucleus. Article 13 defines an avecindado as a Mexican adult who has lived on the ejido for a year or more and been recognized by the assembly or the agrarian court. Article 15 requires Mexican nationality to become an ejidatario. A sale to an outsider is a different transaction, and only after dominio pleno: the assembly authorizes it, the holder asks the Registro Agrario Nacional to cancel the agrarian inscription and issue a title, and that title is recorded in the local Registro Público de la Propiedad. Until that cancellation, article 82 says the land is still ejidal. In the restricted zone, Article 27 of the Constitution still bars a foreigner from direct title even after that conversion, which is why the bank trust in the fideicomiso guide exists.

What has to be in place before I pay a Spanish developer for a home that is not finished?

Additional provision 1 of Ley 38/1999, as modified by Ley 20/2015, applies to promoters of housing who take payments during construction. From the building licence, they must guarantee return of the amounts plus legal interest, with an individual surety-insurance policy or a solidary bank guarantee, and they must take the money through a credit institution into a special account separated from the promoter's other funds. The purchase contract has to name the insurer or guarantor bank and the account. The promoter hands over a guarantee document individualized to the amounts when the contract is granted. On the insurance path, if the build does not start or does not finish on time, the buyer makes a formal demand and, if the promoter has not returned the money within 30 days, claims from the insurer, which must pay within 30 days of that claim. A bank guarantee can lapse if two years pass from the breach without the buyer demanding rescission and return.

What registry document should I have in Spain or Portugal before I wire?

In Spain, a nota simple from the Registro de la Propiedad identifies the property and the holders of registered rights, including full ownership, mortgages and usufruct, and the extent and limits of those rights. Order it from the registry or the Colegio de Registradores, not from the seller's inbox. In Portugal, the certidão permanente of the land registry, on the government service page, is valid for six months, stays updated, and shows the registrations in force and the applications that are still pending. A PDF the seller emails is not either of those documents.

How does a power of attorney become the scam?

The document is an authorization. A special power for one identified property, with a price ceiling and no power to sell or mortgage, is a closing tool. A general power, drafted by someone on the seller's side, that lets the holder receive the price or resell, is a standing authority over the asset and the money. The power-of-attorney guide covers the form each country actually accepts. This page's check is the scope: read who can be paid, what they can sign, and when the power ends, before anyone wires.

Where do I report a wire that already left?

The State Department's scams page says to file a complaint with the Federal Trade Commission and with the FBI at ic3.gov. The 2025 IC3 report describes the Recovery Asset Team opening a Financial Fraud Kill Chain when a complaint comes in quickly: in one August 2025 business-email case tied to a closing, the wire was for over $449,000 and the recipient bank still held the full amount when the freeze was requested. Speed is the fact the report illustrates. It does not promise recovery.
